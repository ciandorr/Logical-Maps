# Atomlessness ⇒ Atoms are strong atoms

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026 (the type-t observation); extended to every relational type by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, the same day; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Atomlessness.** Atomlessness in the displayed closed propositional formulation.

## Conclusion

- **Atoms are strong atoms.** At each relational type, every atom is a strong atom: it necessarily entails every entity of the type or its negation.

## Proof

Vacuously. Atomlessness says that every possible proposition has a possible strict part, so there is no atom of type $t$. Nor is there one of any relational type: the existential projection $\exists\bar x\, .\,I\bar x$ of an atom $I$ of type $\bar\sigma t$ would be an atom of type $t$, since it is not bottom and, for any proposition $p$, the entity $I\land\lambda\bar x\, .\,p$ lies below $I$, so is $I$ or bottom, which puts the projection below $p$ or disjoint from it. So every atom of every relational type is a strong atom.

## Notes

With it Atomlessness gives Atoms are self-atoms at every type, through [[atoms-are-strong-atoms-r-implies-atoms-are-self-atoms-r]]. It gives nothing about self-atoms, which are atoms only at the worlds where they hold; Self-Atomicity fails under Atomlessness through the recorded incompatibility of Atomlessness with Actuality.
