# Atoms are self-atoms ∧ Self-atoms are strong atoms ⇒ Atoms are strong atoms

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Atoms are self-atoms.** At each relational type, every atom is a self-atom: necessarily, if it is instantiated, it is an atom.
- **Self-atoms are strong atoms.** At each relational type, every self-atom is a strong atom: it necessarily entails every entity of the type or its negation.

## Conclusion

- **Atoms are strong atoms.** At each relational type, every atom is a strong atom: it necessarily entails every entity of the type or its negation.

## Proof

An atom is a self-atom by the first premise, and so a strong atom by the second.

## Notes

The converse fails without Self-atoms are atoms: the full model over the free monoid on two generators satisfies Atoms are strong atoms, every singleton being strong, while the self-atom of its two generators is not strong.
