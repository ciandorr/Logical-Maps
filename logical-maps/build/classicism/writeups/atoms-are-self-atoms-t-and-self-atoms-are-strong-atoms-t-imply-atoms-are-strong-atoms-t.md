# Atoms are self-atoms (type t) ∧ Self-atoms are strong atoms (type t) ⇒ Atoms are strong atoms (type t)

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Atoms are self-atoms (type t).** Every atom is a self-atom: necessarily, if it is true, it is an atom.
- **Self-atoms are strong atoms (type t).** Every self-atom is a strong atom.

## Conclusion

- **Atoms are strong atoms (type t).** Every atom is a strong atom.

## Proof

An atom is a self-atom by the first premise, and so a strong atom by the second.

## Notes

The converse fails without Self-atoms are atoms: the full model over the free monoid on two generators satisfies Atoms are strong atoms, every singleton being strong, while the self-atom of its two generators is not strong.
