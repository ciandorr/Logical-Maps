Coalesced sum: exits into pointed components for Σ, definable root

Cian Dorr gave the definable root on 20 October 2026 (coalesced-definable-root). GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Cian Dorr, adapted it on 1 November 2026 to a signature interpreted in the components, with a freely chosen root extension for each constant. Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, wrote this description, the verification and the injectivity argument on 21–22 November 2026. Cian Dorr has checked the construction against the injectivity argument. No Lean verification is claimed.

The construction

Let \Sigma^{+} be \Sigma, with an individual constant d added if \Sigma has none. C(\Sigma^{+}) is conservative over C(\Sigma).

  • Components. A set A of pointed action models for \mathcal L(\Sigma^{+}), with disjoint objects and arrows, complete for C(\Sigma^{+}): every closed sentence consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}) is true at the distinguished object V_{\mathfrak A} of some \mathfrak A\in A.
  • Arrows. Add an object W_0. Its arrows out are its identity and, for each \mathfrak A and each arrow k of \mathfrak A out of V_{\mathfrak A}, an arrow k\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}, where \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}:W_0\to V_{\mathfrak A} is the exit. No arrow enters W_0 except its identity.
  • Composition. k'\circ(k\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}):=(k'k)\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}.
  • Individuals. The individuals at W_0 are the individual constants of \Sigma^{+}; \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} sends each to its denotation at V_{\mathfrak A}. (Closed terms of type e are individual constants, no type ending in e being admitted.)
  • Domains at W_0. By recursion on types. An entity of type \bar\tau t is an intension F over the arrows out of W_0 such that, for some closed term u of \mathcal L(\Sigma^{+}) of that type, the value of F at k\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} is the extension at k of the entity u denotes in \mathfrak A, for every \mathfrak A and k. Its value at the identity is any set E of tuples of root entities of the types \bar\tau. Write F=(E,u), and call E the root extension and u a descriptor.
  • Evaluation point. 1_{W_0}.
  • Interpretation of \Sigma. An individual constant denotes itself. A relational constant c denotes (E_c,c) for any chosen E_c. Its transport along \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} is \mathfrak A’s own interpretation of c.

The differences from coalesced-definable-root: the components interpret the signature and are complete for C(\Sigma^{+}), not only for C; one exit into each pointed component replaces the arrows for every interpretation of d into every object; and the constants of \Sigma are not at the top but keep their own descriptors, with root extensions left to choice.

Descriptors. (E,u) and (E',u') are the same entity iff E=E' and C(\Sigma^{+}) proves u=u'. If u=u' is unprovable, u\ne u' is consistent, so it holds at some V_{\mathfrak A} and the component parts differ. So each root entity has a descriptor that is well defined up to provable identity.

Landing. The transport of (E,u) along \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} is the entity u denotes at V_{\mathfrak A}, so it lies in the component domain.

It is a model of C

As for coalesced-definable-root. Let s be a term with parameters (\bar E,\bar u) from the root domains. At an arrow into \mathfrak A its denotation is computed in \mathfrak A from the transported parameters, which are the denotations of \bar u; so it is the denotation of the closed term s[\bar u], uniformly in the arrow. At the identity its extension is the set of tuples of root entities satisfying it at W_0, and nothing constrains that set. So s denotes the root entity (E',s[\bar u]) for some E'. The axioms of C hold at W_0 because denotations are computed extensionally on intensions, and the other worlds are worlds of the components. Two consequences are used below.

  1. A closed term u denotes an entity with descriptor u, whatever the root extensions E_c are. Only its root extension depends on them.
  2. An application (E,u) of type \sigma t to a root entity x with descriptor v is the root proposition with descriptor uv, true at the identity iff x\in E.

Maximalism (signature Σ) and Possibility+ (signature Σ)

A closed sentence holds at \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} iff it holds at V_{\mathfrak A}. Every closed sentence consistent with C(\Sigma) is consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}), so it holds at some V_{\mathfrak A} and is possible at W_0. A closed identity of \mathcal L(\Sigma) is true at W_0 iff C(\Sigma) proves it, by the descriptor criterion and conservativity. For Possibility+, distinct root individuals distinct from the constants in P are fresh distinct constants, and substituting them for the free variables of a consistent P gives a consistent sentence.

This holds for every choice of the root extensions E_c. The map (E,u)\mapsto[u] is a homomorphism from the root domains onto the closed terms modulo provable identity.

Constants are injective on closed terms

Proposition. Let c be a constant of \Sigma of type \bar\beta\,\sigma\,\bar\beta'\,t, and let A, B be closed terms of \mathcal L(\Sigma) of type \sigma, which may contain c. If

C(\Sigma)\vdash\forall\bar w\,\bar w'\,.\,c[\bar w,A,\bar w']\leftrightarrow c[\bar w,B,\bar w'],

then C(\Sigma)\vdash A=B. In particular, for a constant Q of type \sigma t: C(\Sigma)\vdash QA\leftrightarrow QB only if C(\Sigma)\vdash A=B.

Proof. Suppose C(\Sigma) does not prove A=B; then neither does C(\Sigma^{+}). Take the model above with

E_c:=\{(\bar w,x,\bar w'):\text{the descriptor of }x\text{ is provably identical to }A\}.

This is specified before any denotation is computed, and by consequence 1 the denotation of A has descriptor A and that of B has descriptor B whatever E_c is. So at W_0, c[\bar w,A,\bar w'] is true for all root \bar w,\bar w' and c[\bar w,B,\bar w'] for none. Every root domain is nonempty, so the displayed sentence is false at W_0, which is a model of C(\Sigma). \square

When \sigma=e the statement is trivial, closed terms of type e being constants.

Corollary. Maximalism (signature Σ) proves, for closed A, B and a constant Q of type \sigma\tau with \tau\ne e: QA=QB\to A=B. (If QA=QB is true it is provable, hence so is the displayed biconditional for the constant Q, hence A=B.)

The same choice of root extension shows more generally that a closed term built from c by filling its other arguments is injective on closed terms. This is the property of constants that the Russell–Myhill argument in strong-possibility-signature-r-inconsistent needs.

Other verdicts

  • Actuality. (\text{true},\bot) is true at the identity and nowhere else, and no arrow leads back to W_0; the group’s argument applies.
  • Relational Choice holds by the group’s argument, being evaluated at the identity, where root extensions are unconstrained.
  • Boxed principles. Every pure sentence consistent with C is possible at W_0, so the group’s arguments from that fact apply.
  • The map derives the rest from these and from Maximalism (signature Σ): the failure of ND (directly: (\text{false},\bot) and (\text{true},\bot) are distinct at W_0 and identical along every exit), of Atomicity, and of Strong Possibility (signature Σ); Well-Ordering and Transversal Choice; and the other signature principles.

The root algebra (23 November 2026)

Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, added this section and the argument root-algebra on 23 November 2026.

Let L be the Lindenbaum algebra of C(\Sigma^{+}). A root proposition is (e,[v]) with e its truth value at the identity and v a closed sentence. A closed sentence fails at some world seen from W_0 through an exit iff its negation is consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}), since by necessitation \vdash v iff \vdash\Box v. So at W_0

\Box(e,v)\iff e=1\text{ and }\vdash v,\qquad (e,v)\le(f,w)\iff e\le f\text{ and }\vdash v\to w .

The propositions at W_0 form 2\times L, and every set of them is the root extension of a root property. None of this depends on A or on the E_c.

Boolean Completeness (type t) fails. A greatest lower bound at W_0 is one in 2\times L, so it is enough that L is not complete.

  • \Sigma^{+} countable. Let \varphi_n say that there are exactly n individuals (n\ge1). Each is consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}), true in the full one-world model with n individuals under any interpretation of the constants, and they are pairwise inconsistent. If L were complete, S\mapsto\bigvee\{\varphi_n:n\in S\} would be injective on \mathcal P(\omega), because \varphi_m\wedge\bigvee_{n\in S}\varphi_n=\bigvee_{n\in S}(\varphi_m\wedge\varphi_n) is \varphi_m or \bot as m\in S or not. But L is countable.
  • \Sigma^{+} with infinitely many constants. Suppose first there are infinitely many relational constants c_0,c_1,\ldots, of types \bar\rho_i t. Fix closed terms \bar a_i of types \bar\rho_i not containing them (d at type e, \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot otherwise), and put P_i:=c_i(\bar a_i) and a_i:=P_i\land\bigwedge_{j<i}\neg P_j. These are consistent and pairwise inconsistent. Let w be an upper bound of the a_i with i even, and suppose w mentions only c_j with j<N. For even i>N, substituting \lambda\bar x\, .\,\top for c_i and \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot for c_j (N\le j<i) in a proof of a_i\to w gives \vdash \bigwedge_{j<N}\neg P_j\to w. So a_M\le w for every odd M>N, and w\land\neg a_M is a strictly smaller upper bound. With infinitely many individual constants instead, the same argument works with a_i:=(c_i=c_0)\land\bigwedge_{1\le j<i}c_j\ne c_0 (i\ge1): substitute c_0 for c_i and generalize on the fresh c_j to get \vdash\bigwedge_{1\le j<N}c_j\ne c_0\land\exists y\, .\,y\ne c_0\to w, which every a_M with odd M>N\ge2 entails.

Every infinite signature has infinitely many constants, so the two cases cover every \Sigma. Neither uses choice.

Self-atoms need not be atoms or strong atoms (type t). Let \mathrm F be the Fregean Axiom \forall p\, .\,p=\top\lor p=\bot, and s:=(0,\mathrm F).

  • s is possible at W_0, \mathrm F being consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}) (the full one-world model with one individual).
  • C proves \mathrm F\to\operatorname{Atom}(\mathrm F): under \mathrm F the true proposition \mathrm F is \top, and the only propositions are \top and \bot. So s\to\operatorname{Atom}(s) holds at every exit, and trivially at the identity, where s is false. So s is a self-atom.
  • (0,\mathrm F\land\forall x\forall y\, .\,x=y) lies strictly between \bot and s: \mathrm F holds in the full one-world models with one and with two individuals. So s is not an atom.
  • A strong atom (0,v) at W_0 must have, for each closed w, \vdash v\to w or \vdash v\to\neg w, so v must be an atom of L. \mathrm F is not, by the previous point. So s is not a strong atom.

The map derives the versions at all relational types.

Left open

The map leaves eighteen principles undecided for this model. The record lists only verdicts that hold for every \Sigma and every choice of the E_c, and many of these go different ways for different \Sigma. The facts below were found on 23 November 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, and are not recorded as verdicts.

Fresh constants. The axioms of C(\Sigma^{+}) are schemata, so a constant occurring in no premise can be generalized on: if \vdash\varphi(c) with c not in \varphi, then \vdash\forall x\, .\,\varphi(x). This is what makes the verdicts turn on how many constants \Sigma has.

Principle Fails when Holds when
BF (all types, type t, relational types), Functionality, Tractarianism \Sigma^{+} has finitely many individual constants (BF at type e); or \Sigma=\emptyset, at type t \Sigma^{+} has infinitely many constants of every type
Infinity Schema (type e), Axiom of Infinity (type e) finitely many individual constants infinitely many individual constants (at least the Schema)
Atoms are strong atoms (type t; relational types) \Sigma=\emptyset infinitely many relational constants
  • BF, Functionality, Tractarianism. These are equivalent on the map. For a root X=(E,u) of type \sigma t, \forall x\, .\,\Box Xx holds at W_0 iff every root entity is in E and \vdash uw for every closed w of type \sigma; \Box\forall x\, .\,Xx holds iff, in addition, \vdash\forall x\, .\,ux. With a constant of type \sigma not in u the second follows by generalization. At type e with only c_1,\ldots,c_n, \Diamond\exists x\, .\,x\ne c_1\land\cdots\land x\ne c_n holds and no root individual is possibly such. At type t with \Sigma=\emptyset, the argument barcan of coalesced-definable-root applies unchanged: a closed sentence of \mathcal L(\{d\}) is not contingent at a world with C5, Atomicity and one individual.
  • Infinity at type e. The root individuals are the individual constants of \Sigma^{+}, and distinct constants are distinct at W_0.
  • Atoms are strong atoms. With infinitely many relational constants, L is atomless: if v is consistent and c is a constant not in v, then v\land c(\bar a) and v\land\neg c(\bar a) are consistent (substitute \lambda\bar x\, .\,\top or \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot for c). Then the atoms at a relational type \bar\sigma t at W_0 are the entities with a single tuple as root extension and descriptor \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot (the fresh-constant splitting works at every relational type). These are self-atoms and strong atoms, being \bot along every exit. With \Sigma=\emptyset, v:=\mathrm F\land\forall x\forall y\, .\,x=y is an atom of L, so (0,v) is an atom at W_0. But C does not prove \forall q\, .\,v\le q\lor v\le\neg q. It fails at the root of the coalesced sum of two copies of the one-point Fregean model, where v is the root proposition false at the root and true in both components, and q is true in just one component.

Open for every \Sigma tried:

  • Atoms are self-atoms (type t; relational types). They hold when L is atomless, by the previous point. For \Sigma=\emptyset the question is whether some atom v of L has \vdash v\to\neg\operatorname{Atom}(v). A finite axiomatization of the full S5 model with two worlds and one individual would give one.
  • Tame Rigidity and Weakly Rigid Power. Both fail when \Sigma^{+} has infinitely many individual constants. The root property with every constant in its root extension and descriptor \lambda x\, .\,\top is weakly rigid at W_0, by generalization on a fresh constant. It is rigid only if C proves BF at type e. Weakly Rigid Power implies Tame Rigidity. With finitely many constants, a weakly rigid root relation with finite root extension has a descriptor provably equal to a disjunction of haecceities of closed terms, and so is rigid. The infinite case is open.
  • Rigid Power.
  • Forcing Possibilism, in the formulation of Andrew Bacon’s pull request (dated 16 November 2026, merged 24 November 2026), and Contingency of Cardinal CH, Possible Cardinal CH and Possible failure of Cardinal CH, added in the same revision. These have not been examined for this model. Under the earlier formulation, in which the algebras counted were the finite ones and those with fewer elements than the propositions, Forcing Possibilism failed with infinitely many relational constants and held with \Sigma=\emptyset. With infinitely many relational constants L is atomless, so no interval [\bot,p] has four elements. With \Sigma=\emptyset the atoms \mathrm F\land{}“exactly n individuals” of L give every finite interval. Whether the smallness condition of the new formulation admits the same algebras at W_0 has not been checked.

The group’s shared arguments for Tame Rigidity and Rigid Power transfer a component’s counterexample to the root. They build root entities with freely chosen component parts, which are not available here.
