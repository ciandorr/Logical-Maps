## Definition

As in coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root, with a single component in place of a family complete for C($\Sigma$): the one-object action model $V$ with only its identity arrow, one individual, and the full extensional type hierarchy over it, so that $V$ has exactly two propositions. The root $W_0$ has an arrow $\langle V,I\rangle$ for each interpretation $I$ of $\Sigma^+$ at $V$ and an arrow to itself for each type-preserving map of the constants of $\Sigma^+$. Evaluation point: $W_0$, at its identity arrow. Interpretation of Σ as there. The write-up's argument that the root is a model of C uses only that each component is an action model of C.

As for interpretation arrows, and the root has besides an arrow to itself for every type-preserving map of the signature's constants to themselves, acting on the root's individuals and composing with the interpretation arrows by precomposition.

As in coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root, intensions over the arrows out of $W_0$ pinned down by a finite set of constants of $\Sigma^+$; every transport lands, the component's domains being full.

### Holds Atomlessness, □Atoms are strong atoms. Fails Self-atoms are atoms (type t).

Atomlessness holds at $W_0$ by the argument atomlessness of coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root, which needs of the component only that $\top\ne\bot$ there; so $W_0$ has no atom of type $t$, and by existential projection (as in no-actuality-no-self-atoms) no atom of any relational type, and the bridge principles for atoms hold vacuously there. So both necessitated bridge principles for atoms hold at every type. Self-atoms are atoms (type t) fails at $W_0$: the proposition $P$ true exactly at the arrows into $V$ is pinned down by $\emptyset$, with transport $\top$ along every $\langle V,I\rangle$, so it lands; it is possible, and wherever it is true it is $\top$ at $V$, an atom, so it is a self-atom, but not an atom at the atomless $W_0$.

### Holds □Tame Rigidity, □Rigid Rigidity, □Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity.

The argument tame-rigidity-factorizable, adapted to the two objects. At $V$, which sees only itself, $\Box$ is idle and every relation is weakly rigid and rigid, so all three principles hold there, and it remains to show them at $W_0$. Two arrows out of $W_0$ agree on a finite set $N$ of constants when they are maps agreeing on $N$ or arrows $\langle V,I\rangle,\langle V,I^{\prime}\rangle$ with $I{\restriction}N=I^{\prime}{\restriction}N$. For a set $E$ of entities at $W_0$, each pinned down by a finite set, let $B_N(E)$ be the intension $k\mapsto\bigcup\{k^{\prime}\cdot E:k^{\prime}\text{ agrees with }k\text{ on }N\}$; it is pinned down by $N$ and lands, $V$ being full, so it is in the domain. (1) As there, a relation $Y$ pinned down by $S$ with extension $E$ at the identity is weakly rigid iff $Y=B_N(E)$ for every finite $N\supseteq S$. (2) $Y$ is rigid iff $h\cdot Y$ is weakly rigid for every arrow $h$; for $h$ into $V$ this is automatic, and for a map $h$ it says $k\cdot Y_h\subseteq Y_{kh}\subseteq B_N(Y_h)_k$ for every arrow $k$ and finite $N$. So, as there, every weakly rigid relation is rigid: Tame Rigidity holds at $W_0$. Rigid Rigidity: $R:=\lambda X\,.\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)$ is a closed pure term, so $h\cdot R=R$ for every arrow and by (1) and (2) it suffices that $R=B_N(\mathcal R)$, $\mathcal R$ the rigid relations at $W_0$. $\supseteq$: transports of rigid relations are rigid. $\subseteq$ at a map $k$: a rigid $Z$ pinned down by $T$ is $k^{\prime}\cdot(\sigma\cdot Z)$, where $\sigma$ sends $T$ to fresh constants outside $N$ and $k^{\prime}$ agrees with $k$ on $N$ and sends $\sigma(t)$ back to $t$. $\subseteq$ at $\langle V,I\rangle$: every relation $z$ at $V$ is the transport of a rigid relation, since every element of $V$'s finite full domains is the denotation of a closed pure term, so $z$ is the transport of $\lambda\bar x\,.\,\bigvee_{\bar u\in z}\bar x=\bar t_{\bar u}$, a finite disjunction of haecceities and so rigid. All three are closed sentences true at $W_0$ and at $V$, so their necessitations hold.

### Holds □Intensional Choice.

For each object $U$ and type $\sigma$ fix a well-ordering $<_U$ of the domain $D^\sigma_U$, and let $W$ be the intension of type $\sigma\to\sigma\to t$ with $W(h):={<_{\operatorname{cod}h}}$. Its value depends only on the arrow's codomain, so by the condition it is in the domain, and at every world its extension well-orders that world's type-$\sigma$ domain. Given $F$ with $\Box\exists x\, .\,Fx$, put $G:=\lambda x\, .\,Fx\land\forall y\, .\,(Fy\to\neg Wyx)$, the $W$-least $F$. It is definable from $F$ and $W$, so in the domain; $G\le F$, and its extension at every arrow is the singleton of the least element of $F$'s. So Intensional Choice holds, and the same at every reachable object gives $\Box$Intensional Choice. Cian Dorr's observation that such models are qualitatively full, made precise.

*General argument `arguments/intensional-choice-well-ordering`. ### Holds □Relational Choice, □Transversal Choice.

At any reachable object $W$, let $U$ be a serial relation in the domain there. Using the axiom of choice, pick for each $x$ a $y$ with $(x,y)$ in the extension of $U$ at the identity, and let $S$ be the intension whose value at every arrow $W\to W$ is the graph of this choice and whose value at arrows with any other codomain is empty; it depends only on the codomain, so the condition puts it in the domain, and at the identity it is a functional subrelation of $U$. Likewise a set of representatives of an equivalence relation at $W$, made into such an intension, is a transversal. Since $W$ was any reachable object, both principles hold at every world.

### Holds Necessity of Arithmetic.

At each type $\sigma$. At any object $v$ reachable from the evaluation point the numbers at $\sigma$ are the numerals there, as in the argument arithmetic-necessitism-extensionally-full. If $\sigma$ has infinitely many entities at $v$, the numerals are pairwise distinct there, the $n$th being the cardinality of the properties with $n$ instances; $\operatorname{Sum}_\sigma$ and $\operatorname{Prod}_\sigma$ are the least relations closed under their clauses, and by extensional fullness the relations whose extensions are the graphs of addition and multiplication on the numerals are present and closed under them, so the two are standard. So an arithmetical sentence $A$ at $\sigma$ has at $v$ its truth value in the standard model. If $\sigma$ has finitely many entities at $v$, it has finitely many at every object $v$ sees, by the condition, so two numerals coincide and $I^*_\sigma$ fails at each of them: $I_\sigma=\Diamond I^*_\sigma$ is false at $v$. So $\Box(I_\sigma\to A)$ holds when $A$ is true in the standard model and $\Box(I_\sigma\to\neg A)$ when it is false. No choice is used.

### Holds Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.

Let $v$ be any object reachable from the evaluation point and $\mathfrak R$ a real number structure at $v$ (Background, Analysis). Its completeness is tested against the subproperties of its carrier present at $v$, and by extensional fullness every subset of the carrier's extension there is the extension at the identity of one of them; so the carrier, with the extensions of $\le$, $+$ and $\times$, is a complete ordered field in the sense of the metatheory and, by Huntington's theorem, a theorem of ZF, is isomorphic to the real numbers. For the same reason the extension of $N$, the least subproperty containing $0$ and closed under adding $1$, is its set of natural numbers, that set being the extension of a subproperty. A second-order analytical sentence $A(\mathfrak R)$ quantifies over the relations among entities of the structure's type at $v$, applied to the structure's elements, and their extensions at the identity are all the relations on the carrier, again by extensional fullness. So $A(\mathfrak R)$ holds at $v$ exactly when $A$ holds in the real numbers under full second-order semantics, which depends neither on $v$ nor on $\mathfrak R$. Hence $\Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to A(\mathfrak R)$ holds when $A$ is true of the reals and $\Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to\neg A(\mathfrak R)$ when it is false: every instance of the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis holds, and with it the Necessity of Analysis and the Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis, as recorded.
