# □Rigid Comprehension ∧ Rigid Power ∧ □Axiom of Infinity (type t) ⇒ Necessity of Second-Order Analysis

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Cian Dorr (the idea, 30 October 2026), by Barton and Goodsell's Theorems 3–7; written out by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); recorded by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 30 October 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **□Rigid Comprehension.** Necessarily, every relation, including a proposition, is coextensive with a rigid one.
- **Rigid Power.** If a relation is rigid, so is the property of being a rigid relation that entails it.
- **□Axiom of Infinity (type t).** Necessarily, there are not finitely many propositions.

## Conclusion

- **Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.** Every second-order analytical sentence is either true in every real number structure in every possible world or false in every real number structure in every possible world. One sentence for each type $\sigma$ and each second-order analytical sentence.

## Proof

As for [[necessary-rigid-comprehension-rigid-power-and-necessary-axiom-of-infinity-e-imply-necessity-of-second-order-analysis]], at $\sigma:=t$: the numbers are the finite cardinalities of properties of propositions, and the reals the rigid properties of those numbers. See the write-up.

## Notes

The Axiom of Infinity at $t$ is independent of the one at $e$: under the Fregean Axiom there are two propositions, and a world with finitely many individuals can have infinitely many propositions. Either suffices, since the comparison between real number structures happens within each world. With [[necessary-atomlessness-implies-necessary-axiom-of-infinity-t]], □Atomlessness can stand in for the infinity premise.
