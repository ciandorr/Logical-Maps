# No Pure Contingency ∧ Strong Atomicity ⇒ □Strong Atomicity

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc., Lean `Classicism.Map.no_pure_contingency_and_strong_atomicity_imply_necessary_strong_atomicity`; produced by Cian Dorr, suggestion of 22 September 2026; proof by the pattern of the recorded No Pure Contingency arrows, written out by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), 22 September 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 22 September 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **No Pure Contingency.** Each closed sentence of the pure language, if true, is necessary. This is a sentence schema, not a quantifier over propositions.
- **Strong Atomicity.** At each relational type, every possible entity (one distinct from bottom) is entailed by a strong atom: a possible entity that necessarily entails every entity of the type or its negation. The right-to-left direction is a theorem of C; the principle records the substantive direction.

## Conclusion

- **□Strong Atomicity.** Necessarily, at each relational type, every possible entity (one distinct from bottom) is entailed by a strong atom: a possible entity that necessarily entails every entity of the type or its negation.

## Proof

Every closed instance of Strong Atomicity is a closed sentence of the pure language, since the principle is stated with object-language quantifiers and contains no nonlogical constant. Given Strong Atomicity, each of those instances is true, and No Pure Contingency makes each necessary, which is □Strong Atomicity.
