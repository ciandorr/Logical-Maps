# No Pure Contingency ∧ Strong Atomicity (type t) ⇒ □Strong Atomicity (type t)

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc., Lean `Classicism.Map.no_pure_contingency_and_strong_atomicity_t_imply_necessary_strong_atomicity_t`; produced by Cian Dorr, suggestion of 22 September 2026; proof by the pattern of the recorded No Pure Contingency arrows, written out by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), 22 September 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 22 September 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **No Pure Contingency.** Each closed sentence of the pure language, if true, is necessary. This is a sentence schema, not a quantifier over propositions.
- **Strong Atomicity (type t).** Every possible proposition is entailed by a strong atom, one that is possible and necessarily entails every proposition or its negation.

## Conclusion

- **□Strong Atomicity (type t).** Necessarily, every possible proposition is entailed by a strong atom, one that is possible and necessarily entails every proposition or its negation.

## Proof

Every closed instance of Strong Atomicity (type t) is a closed sentence of the pure language, since the principle is stated with object-language quantifiers and contains no nonlogical constant. Given Strong Atomicity (type t), each of those instances is true, and No Pure Contingency makes each necessary, which is □Strong Atomicity (type t).
