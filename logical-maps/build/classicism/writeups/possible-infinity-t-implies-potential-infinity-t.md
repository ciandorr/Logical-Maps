# Possible Infinity (type t) ⇒ Potential Infinity (type t)

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc., Lean `Classicism.Map.possible_infinity_t_implies_potential_infinity_t`; produced by Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 24 November 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 24 November 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Possible Infinity (type t).** Possibly there are not finitely many propositions: the Axiom of Infinity at this type, under a diamond.

## Conclusion

- **Potential Infinity (type t).** For every natural number, there could be at least that many propositions: each number counted at type $t$ possibly holds of some property of propositions.

## Proof

The Axiom of Infinity at type $t$ implies Potential Infinity there, in C ([[axiom-of-infinity-t-implies-potential-infinity-t]]); necessitating that derivation, Possible Infinity gives $\Diamond\operatorname{PotInf}_t$. And Potential Infinity is true if possible (Background, [[potential-infinity-forms]], part 2): if a number $n$ had $\neg\Diamond\exists X\, .\,nX$, then necessarily $n$ would be a number, numbers being necessarily numbers, and necessarily $\neg\Diamond\exists X\, .\,nX$, by 4, so that Potential Infinity would be impossible.

## Notes

So Potential Infinity (type $t$) is the weakest of the infinity principles at this type that the map records. Whether the converse holds is open; see the notes to [[potential-infinity-t]].
