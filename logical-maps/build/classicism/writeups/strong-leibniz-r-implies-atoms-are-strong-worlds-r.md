# Strong Leibniz Biconditionals ⇒ Atoms are strong worlds

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Strong Leibniz Biconditionals.** At each relational type, every possible entity (one distinct from bottom) is entailed by a strong world: a possible entity that necessarily entails every entity of the type or its negation. The right-to-left direction is a theorem of C; the principle records the substantive direction.

## Conclusion

- **Atoms are strong worlds.** At each relational type, every atom is a strong world: it necessarily entails every entity of the type or its negation.

## Proof

Let $X$ be an atom. It is not bottom, so the Strong Leibniz Biconditionals give a strong world $W\le X$. $W$ is not bottom and is a part of the atom $X$, so $W=X$, and $X$ is a strong world.
