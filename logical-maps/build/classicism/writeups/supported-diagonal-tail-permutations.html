Supported model over Cantor space with diagonal tail permutations

The construction and its verdicts are due to GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Cian Dorr, on 30 October 2026. Astra was answering a brief by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) on whether Intensional Choice implies Transversal. Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, checked it and adapted this text. No independent checker or Lean verification is recorded.

The model satisfies:

  • C5;
  • □BF;
  • □Boolean Completeness (type t);
  • □Atomlessness;
  • □Intensional Choice;
  • Vicinity.

Transversal fails at type t, as do Actuality and Relational Choice. So Intensional Choice does not imply Transversal, even with all those principles together.

The model belongs to the group of supported S5 components, like the supported Boolean-complete model’s component. There Transversal holds, because the symmetry group is all of \operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{RO}(2^\omega)). Two facts about that group give every coextension class a canonical representative:

  • supports intersect;
  • an entity is determined on a whole cell of its support by its restriction to any nonzero piece.

Here the group is much smaller. It is still rich enough for finite attainment, which closes the domains under the quantifiers and gives Intensional Choice by compactness. But an entity’s support is fixed only by permutations that act in the same way in every cell, and that obstructs canonical representatives.

The construction

Let W=2^{\mathbb N}, with coordinates numbered from 1, and let B be its algebra of clopen sets. For a finite binary string s, write [s] for its cylinder.

  • The group. \Gamma_r is the finite group of all permutations of the strings of length r, acting on the first r coordinates and trivially on the rest. Under the natural inclusions, \Gamma=\bigcup_r\Gamma_r is a group of homeomorphisms of W.
  • The diagonal tail groups. For each n, K_n\le\Gamma consists of the maps s^\frown z\mapsto s^\frown h(z) (|s|=n, h\in\Gamma): the same transformation h of the tail in every cylinder of length n. Then K_{n+1}\subseteq K_n,\qquad [\Gamma_r,K_n]=1\quad(n\ge r).\tag{1}
  • The domains. An entity is supported at level n if K_n fixes it. Take D_e=\{*\} and D_t=B, and for a relational type \bar\sigma t D_{\bar\sigma t}=\Bigl\{F:\textstyle\prod_iD_{\sigma_i}\to B\;:\;K_n\text{ fixes }F\text{ for some }n\Bigr\}, \qquad(g\cdot F)(\bar a)=g\bigl(F(g^{-1}\cdot\bar a)\bigr). For the empty argument list this gives B itself, since every clopen set depends on finitely many coordinates. The domains are \Gamma-stable: if K_m fixes F and g\in\Gamma_r, then for n\ge m,r, K_n fixes g\cdot F by (1).
  • The action model. The objects are the points of W, with exactly one arrow between any two. The single individual is fixed by every arrow, and transport is the identity on the domains. The intension of F has at the arrow ending at w the extension \{\bar a:w\in F(\bar a)\}.

In the terms of the group, the support points are B together with the cosets \Gamma/K_n. The stabiliser of the coset K_n is K_n itself. Conversely, the pointwise stabiliser of a finite set of support points contains some K_N: if g\in\Gamma_r, then K_N\subseteq gK_ng^{-1} for N\ge n,r, by (1), and a clopen set depending on the first r coordinates is fixed by K_N for N\ge r. So being supported by a finite set of support points is being fixed by some K_n. The fibres are clopen, so the condition stone-worlds holds.

A model of C

Every K_n-orbit is dense in its cylinder of length n, because \Gamma acts transitively on the strings of each length. Hence:

\text{every }K_n\text{-invariant open subset of }W\text{ is a union of cylinders of length }n.\tag{2}

If such a set meets a cylinder, it contains a point of that cylinder whose orbit is dense in the cylinder, so by openness and invariance it contains every point of the cylinder.

  • Quantifiers. Let F\in D_{\sigma t} be fixed by K_n. Then \exists x\,Fx has the value \bigcup_{a\in D_\sigma}F(a). This set is open, and it is K_n-invariant because K_n permutes D_\sigma and F is equivariant. By (2) it is a finite union of cylinders, so it is clopen. \forall follows by complementation. With further free arguments, fix them and take a common level for them and for F; the resulting relation is fixed by K_n, quantification being equivariant.
  • Term-forming operations. The Boolean operations preserve support. So do application and currying, after passing to a common level, which exists because the K_n are nested. Abstraction preserves support because the logical operations are equivariant.
  • Identity is strict, with values W and \varnothing. This agrees with Leibniz identity, because the crisp singleton of an entity a is supported wherever a is.

So the domains contain the denotations of all terms, and the structure is an action model of C. \Box p is W if p=W and \varnothing otherwise. Identity is rigid, and the domains and transport are constant, so C5 and □BF hold. Atomlessness holds because B is atomless. Actuality fails because no point has a least clopen neighbourhood. Vicinity holds with \top as witness: every true proposition is nonempty, so it is necessarily possible.

Intensional Choice

This is the group’s compactness argument. Let F\in D_{\sigma t} be necessarily instantiated, so that W=\bigcup_aF(a). By compactness, W=F(a_1)\cup\dots\cup F(a_k) for some a_1,\dots,a_k. Disjointify, taking c_i:=F(a_i)\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}F(a_j), and let G(a):=\bigcup\{c_i:a_i=a\}. The finitely many a_i and c_i have a common level, which supports G. G lies below F and has exactly one instance at every world. So □Intensional Choice holds at every type.

Transversal fails at type t

Fix w=000\cdots. For g\in\Gamma, let F_g(p):=g^{-1}[p]. If g\in\Gamma_r, then K_r fixes F_g, by (1). Its extension at w is given by w\in F_g(p)\iff g(w)\in p.

Suppose K_n fixes \Phi\in D_{(tt)t} and \Phi witnesses Transversal at w. Let g flip coordinate n+1, and v:=g(w). Let Y be the unique \Phi-selected property coextensive with F_g at w, so that

w\in Y(p)\iff v\in p\qquad(p\in B).\tag{3}

Step 1: Y is fixed by the elements of K_n that fix w and v. Let L:=\{h\in K_n:h(w)=w,\ h(v)=v\}. Each h\in L fixes \Phi and w, so h\cdot Y is also selected at w. It also satisfies (3), because h(v)=v. By uniqueness, h\cdot Y=Y.

Step 2: an element fixing Y and w but moving v. Choose m\ge n+1 such that K_m fixes Y. Let k\in K_m flip coordinate m+1, and put w':=k(w), v':=k(v). All four points lie in [0^n], and their tails after coordinate n begin as follows, with j:=m-n:

point tail prefix
w 0^{j+2}
v 10^{j+1}
w' 0^j10
v' 10^{j-1}10

Let l\in K_n apply, in every cylinder of length n, the transposition of the tail prefixes 10^{j-1}10 and 10^{j-1}11. Then l fixes w, v and w' and moves v', so l\in L. Since k and l both fix Y, so does s:=k^{-1}lk. Moreover s(w)=w and s(v)\ne v.

Step 3: the contradiction. Applying s\cdot Y=Y to (3) gives, for every clopen p, v\in p\iff w\in Y(p)\iff w\in Y(s^{-1}p)\iff v\in s^{-1}p\iff s(v)\in p. Clopen sets separate the points of Cantor space, so s(v)=v, a contradiction.

So Transversal fails at w, at type t.

Boolean Completeness at type t, necessarily

Fix a world u=a^\frown z with |a|=n, and X\in D_{tt} fixed by K_n. The extension of X at u is S:=\{b:u\in X(b)\}, which is invariant under H:=K_n\cap\operatorname{Stab}(u).

Density. In each cylinder [b] of length n, H fixes b^\frown z, and every other point b^\frown y has an H-orbit dense in [b]. To see this, take a target cylinder. At a long enough level R, the prefixes of y and z differ, and the target contains a string t of length R other than z{\restriction}R. The transposition of y{\restriction}R and t fixes z{\restriction}R.

The least upper bound. The union O:=\bigcup S is open and H-invariant. If it meets a cylinder of length n, it contains a point there other than the fixed one, so by density its closure contains the whole cylinder. So \overline O is a finite union of such cylinders, hence clopen, and it is the least clopen upper bound of S. Greatest lower bounds follow by complementation, \lambda p\,.\,X(\neg p) being in the domain. This is Boolean Completeness for the properties in the domain; the clopen algebra itself is not complete.

Relational Choice fails

Let R(p,q):=(p\land q\land q<p)\lor(\neg p\land q=\bot), a closed term. It is serial at every world, because a true clopen proposition has a proper clopen part containing that world. Suppose a functional S\le R at u is fixed by K_n, and let p be the cylinder of length n containing u. The unique q with S(p,q) at u is fixed by H=K_n\cap\operatorname{Stab}(u), since H fixes S, p and u. But by density an H-invariant clopen part of p is empty or all of p, so it cannot be a proper true part of p.
