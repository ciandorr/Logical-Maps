Stochastic dominance: finite-shift total extension

There is a model of DTU and Shift Invariance in which Shift Transfer fails. For UU uniform on [0,1][0,1], the model makes

A=M1/2(U+1,0)≻B=M1/2(U,1).A=M_{1/2}(U+1,0)\succ B=M_{1/2}(U,1).

Shift Transfer requires indifference, with p=1/2p=1/2 and transfer parameter b=1/2b=1/2. Both gambles are bounded and have expectation 3/43/4.

The canonical DU cone

Take all measurable real-valued random variables on the standing atomless standard probability space, with real outcomes and identity utility. Let VV be the real vector space of survival profiles of finite signed Borel measures of total mass zero, identified almost everywhere. In particular,

dX,Y(t)=SX(t)−SY(t)=FY(t)−FX(t)∈V.d_{X,Y}(t)=S_X(t)-S_Y(t)=F_Y(t)-F_X(t)\in V.

Define

P={v∈V:v≥0 almost everywhere},P=\{v\in V:v\geq0\text{ almost everywhere}\},

N={n∈V:n is compactly supported and finite-step,∫n=0},C=P+N.N=\{n\in V:n\text{ is compactly supported and finite-step},\ \int n=0\}, \qquad C=P+N.

Here “finite-step” means only finitely many steps. For any cone HH, write LH=H∩(−H)L_H=H\cap(-H) for its indifferent subspace and Hstr=H\(−H)H^{\mathrm{str}}=H\setminus(-H) for its strictly positive part.

The cone CC has

$$L_C=N. \tag{1}$$

Indeed, if p+n=−(q+m)p+n=-(q+m) with p,q∈Pp,q\in P and n,m∈Nn,m\in N, then p+q=−(n+m)p+q=-(n+m) is nonnegative with integral zero. Hence p=q=0p=q=0 almost everywhere. Conversely, NN is a subspace contained in CC. The same argument gives the useful criterion

$$v\in L^1,\quad\int v=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad(v\in C\iff v\in N). \tag{2}$$

The ordering X≽CYX\succeq_CY iff dX,Y∈Cd_{X,Y}\in C satisfies DU. Cone addition gives transitivity, and law dependence gives Stochastic Equivalence. Mixing both laws with a common law multiplies their difference by p>0p>0, so positive scalar cancellation proves Mixture Independence in both directions. Nonnegative profiles are in PP; a nonzero one cannot lie in NN, so strict Stochastic Dominance remains strict. A strict threshold inequality gives a nonzero profile almost everywhere by right continuity.

For finite-valued X,YX,Y, the profile dd is compact finite-step, with m=∫d=E[X]−E[Y]m=\int d=E[X]-E[Y]. Writing h=1[0,1)h=\mathbf1_{[0,1)} gives d=mh+(d−mh)d=mh+(d-mh), where d−mh∈Nd-mh\in N. Thus m≥0m\geq0 implies d∈Cd\in C. Conversely, if d=p+n∈Cd=p+n\in C, then p=d−np=d-n is integrable and m=∫p≥0m=\int p\geq0. This proves Simple EU, including strictness for unequal means. Sure outcomes consequently have the ordinary real order and identity chart. All real outcomes are available, and for a>b>ca>b>c the mixture of a,ca,c with weight (b−c)/(a−c)(b-c)/(a-c) on aa is indifferent to bb. Thus Rich Outcomes and Archimedean Outcomes hold.

The earlier finite-support-compensation write-up also proves that this is the least DU preorder on real utility laws. The extension below needs only the directly verified properties above.

Finite-shift conclosure

Let ℱ\mathcal F be the probability laws with finite support. For μ=∑i=1kaiδbi\mu=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i\delta_{b_i}, with ai>0a_i>0 and ∑iai=1\sum_i a_i=1, set

Tμv(t)=∑i=1kaiv(t−bi).T_\mu v(t)=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i v(t-b_i).

These operators map VV to VV, commute, include the identity, and satisfy TμTν=Tμ*νT_\mu T_\nu=T_{\mu*\nu}. Also, for a,b≥0a,b\geq0 with a+b>0a+b>0,

$$aT_\mu+bT_\nu=(a+b)T_{(a\mu+b\nu)/(a+b)}. \tag{3}$$

Every kernel on the right still has finite support. Point masses supply all real shifts and their inverses. No countable averaging or topological closure is used.

Every TμT_\mu preserves CC weakly and strictly: it maps PP to PP and NN to NN. If p∈Pp\in P is nonzero, then TμpT_\mu p is nonnegative and nonzero. Thus Tμ(p+n)T_\mu(p+n) cannot be in NN, since that would put the nonzero nonnegative profile TμpT_\mu p in NN.

For any forward-invariant cone HH, define

Satf(H)={v∈V:∃μ∈ℱ,Tμv∈H}.\operatorname{Sat}_f(H)=\{v\in V:\exists\mu\in\mathcal F,\ T_\mu v\in H\}.

It is a cone. If Tμv,Tνw∈HT_\mu v,T_\nu w\in H, then

Tμ*ν(v+w)=Tν(Tμv)+Tμ(Tνw)∈H.T_{\mu*\nu}(v+w)=T_\nu(T_\mu v)+T_\mu(T_\nu w)\in H.

Positive scalar closure is immediate. For every ρ∈ℱ\rho\in\mathcal F,

$$v\in\operatorname{Sat}_f(H) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T_\rho v\in\operatorname{Sat}_f(H). \tag{4}$$

For the forward implication, commute TρT_\rho past a witnessing operator and use forward invariance of HH. For the reverse implication, compose ρ\rho with the witnessing kernel. Call a cone satisfying (4) saturated.

If the operators also preserve strictness in HH, saturation preserves every strict comparison of HH. For v∈Hstrv\in H^{\mathrm{str}}, membership of −v-v in the saturation would give −Tμv∈H-T_\mu v\in H, contradicting Tμv∈HstrT_\mu v\in H^{\mathrm{str}}.

Therefore D0=Satf(C)D_0=\operatorname{Sat}_f(C) is saturated and preserves all weak and strict comparisons of CC. Its indifferent subspace may be larger than NN; the proof does not identify the two.

A transfer pair that stays incomparable

For the displayed A,BA,B, direct calculation gives

$$v(t)=S_A(t)-S_B(t)= \begin{cases} (t-1)/2,&0<t<1,\\ (2-t)/2,&1<t<2,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \tag{5}$$

almost everywhere. Its positive and negative integrals are each 1/41/4.

For every μ∈ℱ\mu\in\mathcal F, TμvT_\mu v is compactly supported and has integral zero. It is not finite-step. Merge repeated atoms and let b0b_0 be the smallest atom of μ\mu, with weight a0>0a_0>0. Choose δ>0\delta>0 smaller than 11 and smaller than the gap to the next atom, if there is one. On (b0,b0+δ)(b_0,b_0+\delta), only the translate at b0b_0 contributes:

Tμv(t)=a02(t−b0−1).T_\mu v(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}(t-b_0-1).

Its slope is a0/2>0a_0/2>0 on a nonempty interval, so it cannot agree almost everywhere with a finite-step function. Criterion (2) consequently excludes both TμvT_\mu v and −Tμv-T_\mu v from CC. Thus

$$v\notin D_0,\qquad -v\notin D_0. \tag{6}$$

The finiteness of the kernel is used in the existence of the smallest atom and the positive gap.

Orienting a comparison without adding indifferences

Let DD be any saturated cone and suppose w∉D∪(−D)w\notin D\cup(-D). Adjoin ww and all its finite shift averages:

K={d+aTμw:d∈D,a≥0,μ∈ℱ}.K=\{d+aT_\mu w:d\in D,\ a\geq0,\ \mu\in\mathcal F\}.

Identity (3) makes KK a cone. Commutation and forward invariance of DD make it forward invariant. We will prove

$$L_K=L_D,\qquad L_{\operatorname{Sat}_f(K)}=L_D. \tag{7}$$

First suppose k=d1+aTμwk=d_1+aT_\mu w and −k=d2+bTνw-k=d_2+bT_\nu w belong to KK. If a+b>0a+b>0, adding gives

0=d1+d2+(a+b)Tθw0=d_1+d_2+(a+b)T_\theta w

for a finite probability law θ\theta, by (3). Hence −Tθw∈D-T_\theta w\in D; saturation would force −w∈D-w\in D, a contradiction. Thus a=b=0a=b=0, and k∈LDk\in L_D. The reverse inclusion follows from D⊆KD\subseteq K.

Now suppose x,−x∈Satf(K)x,-x\in\operatorname{Sat}_f(K). Choose witnesses with Tμx∈KT_\mu x\in K and −Tνx∈K-T_\nu x\in K. Forward invariance and commutation give both Tμ*νxT_{\mu*\nu}x and its negative in KK. By the first equality in (7), Tμ*νx∈LDT_{\mu*\nu}x\in L_D. Applying saturation of DD to both signs gives x∈LDx\in L_D. The reverse inclusion is again immediate.

Consequently D′=Satf(K)D'=\operatorname{Sat}_f(K) is saturated, extends DD, and has exactly the same indifferent subspace. Every old strictly positive element stays strict: acquiring its negative would put it in LD′=LDL_{D'}=L_D. Also w∈D′w\in D' and w∉LDw\notin L_D, so ww is strictly positive in D′D'. This proves strictness after saturation, not merely before it.

Apply this lemma to D0D_0 and the profile vv from (5). Write D1D_1 for the resulting saturated cone and L=LD0=LD1L=L_{D_0}=L_{D_1}. Then vv is strict in D1D_1 and all original strict comparisons of CC remain strict.

Total extension

Consider the family of saturated cones EE containing D1D_1 and satisfying E∩(−E)=LE\cap(-E)=L, ordered by inclusion. It is nonempty, since it contains D1D_1. The union of any nonempty chain is a saturated cone: each cone operation involves finitely many memberships, and each direction of (4) holds in the chain member containing the relevant vector. Its indifferent subspace is exactly LL. Indeed, if both xx and −x-x enter the union, they occur together in one chain member, so x∈Lx\in L. The empty chain has upper bound D1D_1.

Zorn’s lemma therefore supplies a maximal member E*E_*. If some w∉E*∪(−E*)w\notin E_*\cup(-E_*), the orientation lemma would supply a strictly larger saturated cone with the same indifferent subspace LL, still containing D1D_1. This contradicts maximality. Hence

E*∪(−E*)=V.E_*\cup(-E_*)=V.

Define preferences on the original random variables by

X≽*Y⇔SX−SY∈E*.X\succeq_*Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S_X-S_Y\in E_*.

The cone gives reflexivity and transitivity, and the displayed coverage gives Totality. Law dependence and positive scalar cancellation give Stochastic Equivalence and the full Mixture Independence biconditional. The extension preserves every weak and strict comparison of CC, so Stochastic Dominance, Simple EU, Rich Outcomes, and Archimedean Outcomes remain valid. These are DTU.

For every real bb, equation (4) applied to δb\delta_b gives

X≽*Y⇔X+b≽*Y+b,X\succeq_*Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X+b\succeq_*Y+b,

which is Shift Invariance. The fixed indifferent subspace protects the strict comparison v∈D1\Lv\in D_1\setminus L, so A≻*BA\succ_*B. Shift Transfer therefore fails. Starting the orientation lemma with −v-v instead gives another such model with B≻*AB\succ_*A.

This is a total preorder on gambles; distinct same-law variables remain indifferent. Equivalence classes, rather than individual random variables, carry the associated total order.

Other verified properties and failures

Equation (4) also gives invariance under addition of an independent finite-valued summand. This is already forced by the target axioms: Shift Invariance transfers a weak or strict comparison to every shifted pair, and finite mixture replacement combines the comparisons. Totality supplies the converse by ruling out the reverse strict comparison. No claim of Independent Sum Invariance for arbitrary summands follows from this argument.

Use the same fair coin and UU to couple A,BA,B. On its first branch, A=U+1A=U+1 and B=UB=U; on the other, A=0A=0 and B=1B=1. Thus A−BA-B takes just 1,−11,-1 with equal probabilities. Its mean is zero, while A≻*BA\succ_*B. This refutes Simple Relative Expectation and Relative Expectation. The bounded equal-mean pair also refutes Expected Utility; its equal finite signed areas refute CDF-Area Extension. Simple EU is unaffected, since these mixture laws are not finite-valued.

For every ε>0\varepsilon>0, the profile d=SB+ε−SAd=S_{B+\varepsilon}-S_A is compactly supported, piecewise affine, and has integral ε\varepsilon. Choose a compact finite-step lower approximation q≤dq\leq d with integral m>0m>0; a sufficiently fine partition including the finitely many breakpoints supplies one. With n=q−m1[0,1)n=q-m\mathbf1_{[0,1)}, we have n∈Nn\in N and d−n≥0d-n\geq0. Thus B+ε≽*AB+\varepsilon\succeq_*A for every positive ε\varepsilon, but $B\not\succeq_*A$. This refutes Continuity under Vanishing Shifts. Taking Bn=B+1/nB_n=B+1/n gives E|Bn−B|=1/nE|B_n-B|=1/n, directly refuting the recorded upper-section L¹ Continuity as well.

Symmetric Neutrality fails: together with the verified assumptions it would imply Shift Transfer by the recorded neutrality-and-shift argument. Reflection Anti-Invariance also fails, since with Totality and law invariance it makes every symmetric gamble neutral, contradicting that failure. These are deductions from proved implications, not from the omission of symmetry conditions in the construction. Positive affine invariance is left unasserted.

The construction is nonconstructive and covers all real laws, including those without defined expectations. The accompanying exact-rational check verifies the witness, its areas, simple coupling, finite-kernel examples, and positive-shift margins. It does not compute the maximal ordering or prove the universal extension lemma. The latter is the analytic argument above; no independent checker or Lean verification is claimed.
