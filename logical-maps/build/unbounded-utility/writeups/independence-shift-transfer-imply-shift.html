Mixture Independence and Shift Transfer imply Shift Invariance

Claim. Mixture Independence + Shift Transfer imply Shift Invariance.

Fix real-utility variables X,YX,Y and b∈ℝb\in\mathbb R such that X+bX+b and Y+bY+b are available. Apply Shift Transfer with mixture weight 1/21/2 and transfer parameter b/2b/2, first to (X,X)(X,X) and then to (Y,X)(Y,X):

M1/2(X+b,X)∼M1/2(X,X+b),M_{1/2}(X+b,X)\sim M_{1/2}(X,X+b), M1/2(Y+b,X)∼M1/2(Y,X+b).M_{1/2}(Y+b,X)\sim M_{1/2}(Y,X+b).

Transitivity of the standing preorder lets an indifferent object replace either side of a weak comparison. Therefore

X≽Y⇔M1/2(X,X+b)≽M1/2(Y,X+b)⇔M1/2(X+b,X)≽M1/2(Y+b,X)⇔X+b≽Y+b.\begin{aligned} X\succeq Y &\iff M_{1/2}(X,X+b)\succeq M_{1/2}(Y,X+b)\\ &\iff M_{1/2}(X+b,X)\succeq M_{1/2}(Y+b,X)\\ &\iff X+b\succeq Y+b. \end{aligned}

The first and last equivalences are exactly Mixture Independence, with common second arguments X+bX+b and XX respectively.

No branch interchange, independent copy, totality assumption or law identity occurs. Using XX as the common gamble also avoids assuming the availability of a new sure outcome at utility bb: all transformations used were already stipulated to exist in this instance of Shift Invariance.

Original work: direct consequence. This is a two-application specialization followed by mixture cancellation. The choice of common gamble is the small domain check needed to avoid unnecessary outcome-richness assumptions. No literature novelty is asserted.
