Lexicographic expectation: nonatomic mass

On the full measurable domain, Countable Sure-Thing and Simple EU do not imply Expected Utility, and Countable Sure-Thing and Archimedean Outcomes do not imply Archimedean Gambles. One model refutes both implications, even with Totality, Stochastic Equivalence, Stochastic Dominance, Mixture Independence and Sure-Thing.

Source and work accounting. Zachary Goodsell proposed the two implications on 8 September 2026 and requested proofs and a Russell–Isaacs source check on 13 September. GPT-6 (Codex) supplied the countermodel and proofs below on 13 September 2026. The source comparison at the end distinguishes this new countermodel from Russell and Isaacs’s published discrete-lottery theorem. No independent checker, literature priority or Lean verification is claimed.

Construction

Take outcomes O=[0,1]O=[0,1], with the usual order, Borel structure and reference outcomes 0,10,1. All measurable OO-valued random variables on the standing atomless standard probability space are available.

For any finite positive Borel measure μ\mu on OO, put

Dμ={x:μ({x})>0},c(μ)=μ(O\Dμ).D_\mu=\{x:\mu(\{x\})>0\},\qquad c(\mu)=\mu(O\setminus D_\mu).

Thus DμD_\mu is countable and c(μ)c(\mu) is the total mass of the nonatomic part, including any singular continuous part. For a gamble XX, define

V(X)=(E[X],c(ℒ(X))),X≽Y⇔V(X)≥lexV(Y).V(X)=\bigl(E[X],\,c(\mathcal L(X))\bigr),\qquad X\succeq Y\ \Longleftrightarrow\ V(X)\geq_{\mathrm{lex}}V(Y).

The first coordinate decides unless the means agree; in that case, the larger nonatomic mass is preferred. Both coordinates lie in [0,1][0,1]. Lexicographic order is a total order on these vectors, so its pullback is a reflexive, transitive, total preference on all gambles.

Countable additivity of the two coordinates

Suppose μ=∑nμn\mu=\sum_n\mu_n is a finite positive measure. Then

$$D_\mu=\bigcup_nD_{\mu_n},\qquad c(\mu)=\sum_n c(\mu_n). \tag{1}$$

The first identity follows by evaluating singletons. For each nn, Dμ\DμnD_\mu\setminus D_{\mu_n} is countable and has μn\mu_n-measure zero. Consequently μn(O\Dμ)=c(μn)\mu_n(O\setminus D_\mu)=c(\mu_n); summing gives (1). Also c(aμ)=ac(μ)c(a\mu)=a c(\mu) for a≥0a\geq0. Thus cc is additive under arbitrary countable positive mixtures, not only mixtures with disjoint supports.

Let (En)(E_n) be any countable measurable partition and write μn(B)=P(En∩{X∈B})\mu_n(B)=P(E_n\cap\{X\in B\}). Its sum is ℒ(X)\mathcal L(X), while

ℒ(X|En)=μn+(1−P(En))δ0.\mathcal L(X|E_n)=\mu_n+(1-P(E_n))\delta_0.

Adding an atom at zero changes neither the first moment nor the nonatomic mass. Therefore

$$V(X)=\sum_n V(X|E_n). \tag{2}$$

Each coordinate of this series is nonnegative and summable. In particular, the corresponding difference series for any X,YX,Y is absolutely convergent. The argument permits arbitrary partitions depending on X,YX,Y.

Countable Sure-Thing and Sure-Thing

Suppose X|En≽Y|EnX|E_n\succeq Y|E_n for every nn. Write V(X|En)−V(Y|En)=(an,bn)V(X|E_n)-V(Y|E_n)=(a_n,b_n). Lexicographic nonnegativity means an≥0a_n\geq0, and bn≥0b_n\geq0 whenever an=0a_n=0.

If some an>0a_n>0, then ∑nan>0\sum_n a_n>0, giving X≻YX\succ Y by (2). Otherwise every an=0a_n=0, every bn≥0b_n\geq0, and (2) gives X≽YX\succeq Y. If one local comparison is strict in this second case, some bn>0b_n>0, so the global comparison is strict. This proves both clauses of Countable Sure-Thing exactly as formulated using masked acts in the map.

For a two-cell partition, (2) gives V(X)=V(X|E)+V(X|Ec)V(X)=V(X|E)+V(X|E^c). If X|E∼Y|EX|E\sim Y|E, their vectors agree, so subtracting those equal vectors proves the biconditional in Sure-Thing.

Simple EU, dominance and mixtures

Every simple law has nonatomic mass zero. Thus simple gambles are compared exactly by their ordinary means. Sure outcomes have vector (x,0)(x,0), and a Bernoulli gamble Mp(1,0)M_p(1,0) has vector (p,0)(p,0). The normalized utility chart is uniquely u(x)=xu(x)=x: its middle branch forces p=xp=x; its outer branches cannot calibrate an outcome in [0,1][0,1] at a level outside [0,1][0,1]. The chart is measurable and order-preserving, and satisfies the standing regularity contract. This proves Simple EU, Restricted Totality and Restricted Stochastic Equivalence. Rich Outcomes fails, since level 22 is absent.

For a>b>ca>b>c in [0,1][0,1], take p=(b−c)/(a−c)p=(b-c)/(a-c). Both the sure outcome bb and Mp(a,c)M_p(a,c) have vector (b,0)(b,0), proving Archimedean Outcomes.

Dependence only on the law proves Stochastic Equivalence. By (1), for 0<p<10<p<1,

V(Mp(X,Z))=pV(X)+(1−p)V(Z).V(M_p(X,Z))=pV(X)+(1-p)V(Z).

The difference between two mixtures with a common branch is therefore p(V(X)−V(Y))p(V(X)-V(Y)). Positive multiplication preserves and reflects lexicographic order, proving Mixture Independence.

Finally, weak stochastic dominance on [0,1][0,1] gives E[X]≥E[Y]E[X]\geq E[Y] by the bounded tail-integral formula. If a tail inequality is strict at one threshold, right continuity makes the mean inequality strict. If no tail inequality is strict, the laws agree, so both coordinates agree. Hence Stochastic Dominance, including its strict clause, holds. It also yields Statewise Dominance.

The two counterexamples

Let UU be uniform on [0,1][0,1]. Then

V(U)=(1/2,1),V(1/2)=(1/2,0).V(U)=(1/2,1),\qquad V(1/2)=(1/2,0).

Thus U≻1/2U\succ1/2 despite equal finite expectations. The unique normalized chart is the identity, so choosing another chart cannot restore Expected Utility. This refutes Countable Sure-Thing + Simple EU ⇒ Expected Utility.

Also 1≻U≻01\succ U\succ0. For every p∈(0,1)p\in(0,1),

V(Mp(1,0))=(p,0)≠(1/2,1)=V(U).V(M_p(1,0))=(p,0)\ne(1/2,1)=V(U).

No such mixture is indifferent to UU. This refutes Countable Sure-Thing + Archimedean Outcomes ⇒ Archimedean Gambles.

The bounded outcome space is permitted because neither proposed arrow assumes Rich Outcomes. This is not a model of DU, which does require Rich Outcomes. Under DU or DTU, the recorded St Petersburg obstruction makes Countable Sure-Thing incompatible with the background; that is distinct from a countermodel to either implication with empty background.

Affine maps, transfer and simple differences

Write cX=c(ℒ(X))c_X=c(\mathcal L(X)). Any injective affine map preserves atoms and their masses, so, whenever the transformed gamble stays in [0,1][0,1],

V(aX+b)=(aE[X]+b,cX)(a≠0).V(aX+b)=(aE[X]+b,c_X)\qquad(a\ne0).

For a>0a>0, this preserves and reflects lexicographic comparisons. Thus Positive Affine Invariance, Shift Invariance and Scale Invariance hold. Shift Transfer also holds: both of its mixtures have vector

(pE[X]+(1−p)E[Y]+b,pcX+(1−p)cY).\bigl(pE[X]+(1-p)E[Y]+b,\;pc_X+(1-p)c_Y\bigr).

For Simple Relative Expectation, suppose X−YX-Y has finite range. Partition the space by its values dd. On each cell, the subprobability laws of XX and YY are translates by dd, so their nonatomic masses agree. Summing over these cells using (1) gives cX=cYc_X=c_Y. Consequently X≽YX\succeq Y iff E[X−Y]≥0E[X-Y]\geq0, as required.

Negative Affine Anti-Invariance fails. Take X=UX=U, Y=1/2Y=1/2 and the eligible map x↦1−xx\mapsto1-x. Both before and after transformation, the uniform law strictly exceeds the sure half. In particular, X≽YX\succeq Y but $1-Y\not\succeq1-X$.

Expectation rules and continuity

The same equal-mean pair U≻1/2U\succ1/2 refutes Relative Expectation, CDF-Area Extension and Folded Expectation. Everything is bounded: the relative difference is integrable; both CDF areas are finite and equal; and the folded values are their ordinary means, both 1/21/2. In each case the axiom would require indifference.

L¹ Continuity fails. Let XnX_n be sure 1/2+1/(n+3)1/2+1/(n+3), let XX be sure 1/21/2, and put Y=UY=U. Then

Xn≻Yfor all n,E|Xn−X|=1n+3→0,X_n\succ Y\quad\text{for all }n,\qquad E|X_n-X|=\frac1{n+3}\longrightarrow0,

but $X\not\succeq Y$. Every gamble in this example is available.

Uniqueness of negative self-similarity

Suppose X∼Mp(−aX+b,Z)X\sim M_p(-aX+b,Z), with the transformed gamble available. Equality of the two coordinates gives

E[X]=pb+(1−p)E[Z]1+pa,cX=cZ.E[X]=\frac{pb+(1-p)E[Z]}{1+pa},\qquad c_X=c_Z.

Here a>0a>0 and 0<p<10<p<1, so both divisions used to solve the equations are valid. The same equations fix V(Y)V(Y) whenever Y∼Mp(−aY+b,Z)Y\sim M_p(-aY+b,Z). Thus X∼YX\sim Y, proving Uniqueness of Negative Self-Similarity. Existence of a solution is not part of this principle.

No fixed copula works

In fact Existential Copula Sum Invariance fails, as does its Universal counterpart. The same parameters work for every copula.

Fix any copula CC and realize (U,W)(U,W) with joint law CC on the standing sample space. Take

X=U/4,Y=1/8,Z=W/4.X=U/4,\qquad Y=1/8,\qquad Z=W/4.

The pair (X,Z)(X,Z) admits CC by positive rescaling. The pair (Y,Z)(Y,Z) also admits CC: the CDF of the constant YY is either zero or one, and every copula satisfies HC(0,v)=0H_C(0,v)=0 and HC(1,v)=vH_C(1,v)=v.

As before X≻YX\succ Y, and both sums have mean 1/41/4. But Y+ZY+Z is uniform on [1/8,3/8][1/8,3/8], so

cY+Z=1≥cX+Z.c_{Y+Z}=1\geq c_{X+Z}.

Therefore Y+Z≽X+ZY+Z\succeq X+Z while $Y\not\succeq X$, contradicting SC(C)\mathrm{SC}(C). This uses the comparison in the reversed order (Y,X)(Y,X), which the universal quantifier permits. Since CC was arbitrary, no copula satisfies SC(C)\mathrm{SC}(C). No assumption about atoms of X+ZX+Z, and no choice of parameters depending on CC, is needed.

Principles with trivial or empty scope here

The outcome chart is [0,1][0,1]. Numerical axioms concern only their stated eligible gambles and transformations. This matters for the following satisfactions.

  • Reflection Anti-Invariance holds because XX and −X-X can both take values in [0,1][0,1] only if X=0X=0. Every eligible comparison is between zero gambles. This does not imply negative affine anti-invariance: reflection about 1/21/2 is available and fails as shown above, whereas the recorded reflection principle fixes the origin 00.
  • Symmetric Gambles Are Neutral holds because a nonnegative law symmetric about zero is concentrated at zero. Stochastic Equivalence then gives indifference to sure zero.
  • Alternating St Petersburg = −1/2, Pasadena = ln 2 and Arroyo = ln 2 hold vacuously. Their prescribed distributions put positive mass outside [0,1][0,1]: already their first values are −2-2, 22 and 22, respectively. None is an available gamble. The first axiom’s target outcome −1/2-1/2 is absent as well.
  • Continuity under Vanishing Shifts, as currently defined in the map, holds vacuously. Its antecedent requires an available X+ε≽YX+\varepsilon\succeq Y for every ε>0\varepsilon>0. Taking ε=2\varepsilon=2 makes availability impossible. Requiring comparisons only for sufficiently small available shifts would be a different axiom and would fail here, as the L¹ example shows. The recorded principle and its quantifiers have not been changed.

Relation to Russell and Isaacs

Russell and Isaacs, Infinite Prospects (2021), pass to discrete lotteries in §4 and state their Equivalence Theorem in §5. Appendix A defines lotteries as probability mass functions and preferences as total preorders. The bridge from acts to lotteries uses conditional-law dependence; see the author’s April 2020 manuscript, pp. 15–16 and footnotes 18–20, p. 22 and footnote 29, and Appendix A, pp. 25–26: author’s manuscript.

In this model, c(ℒ(X))=0c(\mathcal L(X))=0 for every countably supported lottery. Its restriction to that domain is ordinary bounded expected utility and satisfies Archimedean Gambles. Only nonatomic laws introduce the extra coordinate. Thus the countermodel does not contradict the published discrete-lottery theorem; that theorem does not establish these two arrows on the map’s larger domain.
