Relative Expectation, Neutrality and Shift Invariance imply Folded Expectation

Claim. Rich Outcomes + Stochastic Equivalence + Relative Expectation + Symmetric Neutrality + Shift Invariance imply Folded Expectation.

Write the real utility variable of a gamble as XX. Put

hX(t)=ℙ(X>t)−ℙ(X<−t),F(X)=∫0∞hX(t)dt,h_X(t)=\mathbb P(X>t)-\mathbb P(X<-t),\qquad F(X)=\int_0^\infty h_X(t)\,dt,

and suppose ∫0∞|hX(t)|dt<∞\int_0^\infty|h_X(t)|\,dt<\infty. Let SX(t)=ℙ(X>t)S_X(t)=\mathbb P(X>t), and set d(t)=SX(t)−S−X(t)d(t)=S_X(t)-S_{-X}(t).

For positive tt, d(t)=hX(t)d(t)=h_X(t). For negative tt, d(t)=hX(−t)d(t)=h_X(-t) except possibly when tt or −t-t is an atom threshold. There are at most countably many such thresholds, so

$$\int_{\mathbb R}|d(t)|\,dt=2\int_0^\infty|h_X(t)|\,dt<\infty, \qquad \int_{\mathbb R}d(t)\,dt=2F(X). \tag{1}$$

Let UU be uniform and QXQ_X an increasing quantile for XX. Define

A=QX(U),B=−QX(1−U).A=Q_X(U),\qquad B=-Q_X(1-U).

Arbitrary finite values may be assigned at the null quantile endpoints. These have the laws of XX and −X-X, and both are nondecreasing functions of UU. Their upper-threshold events are therefore nested for every threshold, up to null sets. Tonelli’s theorem and the elementary interval-length identity give

𝔼|A−B|=∫ℝ𝔼|1{A>t}−1{B>t}|dt=∫ℝ|SX(t)−S−X(t)|dt<∞.\begin{aligned} \mathbb E|A-B| &=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbb E\left|\mathbf1_{\{A>t\}}-\mathbf1_{\{B>t\}}\right|\,dt\\ &=\int_{\mathbb R}|S_X(t)-S_{-X}(t)|\,dt<\infty. \end{aligned}

Since this integral is finite, Fubini also gives 𝔼(A−B)=∫d=2F(X)\mathbb E(A-B)=\int d=2F(X).

Now set

Z=A+B2=QX(U)−QX(1−U)2.Z=\frac{A+B}{2}=\frac{Q_X(U)-Q_X(1-U)}2.

Replacing UU by 1−U1-U negates ZZ without changing its law. Thus Symmetric Neutrality gives Z∼0Z\sim0. Moreover, A−Z=(A−B)/2A-Z=(A-B)/2 is integrable with mean F(X)F(X). Relative Expectation applied in both directions gives

A∼Z+F(X),A\sim Z+F(X),

because their actual difference has mean zero. Shift Invariance transports Z∼0Z\sim0 to Z+F(X)∼F(X)Z+F(X)\sim F(X), and Stochastic Equivalence gives X∼AX\sim A. Consequently

X∼F(X).X\sim F(X).

The same argument applies to any other foldable YY. The standing linear sure-outcome order then yields X≽YX\succeq Y exactly when F(X)≥F(Y)F(X)\geq F(Y).

Rich Outcomes supplies the midpoint, shifted variables and sure folded values. All are finite-chart variables, even when their individual ordinary expectations are undefined. No conditional improper integral is used: the absolute integrability in (1) is what justifies both Tonelli/Fubini steps. Totality, Mixture Independence and full affine symmetry are absent from the proof.

Original work: proof adaptation. The source’s folded-expectation argument uses a comparison with a symmetric prospect.
