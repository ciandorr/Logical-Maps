# A small example: properties of a relation

Let R be a binary relation on a nonempty set S. The principles describe
reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, being an equivalence relation, and
connectedness on distinct objects.

An equivalence relation is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. The map records
each forward implication and the converse implication from their conjunction.
Hover over an arrow to follow its direction; click it to inspect the argument.

The identity relation on two objects is an equivalence relation but is not
connected. This is a countermodel to “Equivalence ⇒ Connected”. The strict
two-object chain supplies a different combination of properties. Open Theory
explorer and click either model to inspect its properties and evidence.

The example deliberately leaves “Symmetric ∧ Connected ⇒ Transitive” unanswered
by its records. It illustrates the Conjectures tab; **it is not presented as an
open research problem**. “Open” means unresolved in the supplied database.
Add a valid proof or countermodel as an exercise.

Run `python topics/example/checks/relations.py` from the starter folder to check
the model properties and the recorded proved implications on relations with up
to three objects. The universal implications also have elementary proofs in
their records; finite enumeration alone is not their proof.
