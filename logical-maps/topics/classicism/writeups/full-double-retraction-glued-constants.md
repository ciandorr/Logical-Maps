# Full action model: double retraction with free sites, glued constants

This write-up builds a model of C in which Witnessed Possibility holds while No Pure Contingency fails, and with it Separated Structure (equivalently Possibly Witnessed Possibility) and Strong Actuality. Logical Necessity is the conjunction of Witnessed Possibility with No Pure Contingency; the glued one-object models satisfy both, and the coalesced sums of Classicism, Appendix E, satisfy Witnessed Possibility without No Pure Contingency but keep Separated Structure and Strong Actuality. The model here keeps Bacon's gluing (Logical Combinatorialism, Appendix A.3; book, Definition 18.15) at a root object and uses a second object to break the pure necessities. The recipe is general (§4).

Throughout, $\Sigma$ is the map's fixed countable signature of relational nonlogical constants, and the model is a full action model (Classicism, §3.5; Appendix D): the worlds seen from an object are the arrows out of it, $u\circ h$ is the composite, a proposition at an object is a set of arrows out of it, the transport of a proposition $p$ along $h$ is $\{x : x\circ h\in p\}$, and every intension of every relational type is present at every object. The truth value of a closed pure sentence at an arrow depends only on the arrow's target, since transport along the arrow is a homomorphism of the pure structure.

## 1. The category

Let $\mathcal C$ be the category of the model *two-object retract with two retractions*: objects $W_0,W_1$, generated by $h:W_0\to W_1$ and $j_1,j_2:W_1\to W_0$ with $j_i\circ h=1_{W_0}$. Its arrows are $1_{W_0}$, $h$, $j_1$, $j_2$, $1_{W_1}$ and the idempotents $e_i:=h\circ j_i$, with $e_i\circ e_k=e_k$, $j_i\circ e_k=j_k$ and $e_i\circ h=h$.

Let $F$ be the free monoid on the generators $f_1,f_2,\ldots$, and let $\mathcal D:=\mathcal C *_{W_0}F$ be the category obtained by freely adjoining $F$ as endo-arrows at $W_0$: the pushout, in the category of small categories, of $\mathcal C$ and the one-object category $F$ over the object $W_0$.

**Lemma 1 (normal forms).** Every arrow of $\mathcal D$ is uniquely a composable alternating word of *syllables*, each either a non-identity arrow of $\mathcal C$ or a non-identity element of $F$ (which begins and ends at $W_0$), adjacent syllables being of different kinds. Composites are computed by concatenating and reducing at the junction: two adjacent syllables of the same kind are multiplied in $\mathcal C$ or in $F$, and deleted if the product is an identity, until the word is alternating.

*Proof.* As for free products of monoids: the alternating words with this multiplication form a category, which satisfies the universal property of the pushout, and the inclusion of the words is injective. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (cancellation).** Let $x=x_n\cdots x_1$ and $y=y_1\cdots y_m$ be reduced, with $x_1$ and $y_1$ the syllables applied first. If $x\circ y$ is an identity then $n=m$ and $x_i\circ y_{m+1-i}$ is an identity, in $\mathcal C$ or in $F$, for each $i$.

*Proof.* At the junction, $x_1\circ y_m$: if the two syllables are of different kinds the concatenation is already reduced and has $n+m\ge1$ syllables, so it is not an identity; if they are of the same kind and their product $z$ is not an identity, the word $x_n\cdots x_2\,z\,y_{m-1}\cdots y_1$ is reduced and non-empty. So $x_1\circ y_m$ is an identity, and induction on $n+m$ finishes. $\square$

**Corollary 3 (retractions and isomorphisms).** (a) An arrow has a retraction iff each of its syllables has a left inverse in its own factor, and its retractions are the composites, in reverse order, of such left inverses. (b) In $\mathcal C$, $h$ has exactly the left inverses $j_1,j_2$; $j_i$ has none ($x\circ j_i=1_{W_1}$ would need $x=h$, but $h\circ j_i=e_i$); $e_i$ has none ($x\circ e_i\in\{e_i\}$ for $x\in\{1_{W_1},e_1,e_2\}$); and $F$ has no non-identity element with a one-sided inverse. Hence the only arrow out of $W_0$ other than its identity with a retraction is $h$, whose retractions are exactly $j_1$ and $j_2$, and no non-identity arrow out of $W_1$ has a retraction. (c) The only isomorphisms of $\mathcal D$ are the identities.

**Lemma 4 (sites and factorization).** For each $k$, the map $u\mapsto u\circ f_k$ on arrows out of $W_0$ is injective, and the sets $\{u\circ f_k : u\}$ are pairwise disjoint over $k$: the syllable of $u\circ f_k$ applied first is an $F$-word whose last letter is $f_k$, and deleting that letter recovers $u$. Every arrow $W_0\to W_1$ factors as $h\circ v$ with $v:W_0\to W_0$: its last-applied syllable is an arrow of $\mathcal C$ into $W_1$, that is $h$ or $e_i=h\circ j_i$.

## 2. The glued constants and Witnessed Possibility

Take the full action model over $\mathcal D$ with a single individual at each object, evaluated at $1_{W_0}$. Enumerate as $P_1,P_2,\ldots$ the pure formulas $P(\bar x)$ whose free variables match a tuple $\bar c$ of distinct constants of $\Sigma$ and whose existential closure is true at $1_{W_0}$; this truth does not depend on $\Sigma$. For each $k$ choose a witness tuple $\bar a_k$ from the domains at $W_0$. For a constant $c$ matched by $P_k$ to the component $a$ of $\bar a_k$, let its intension $I_c$ at $W_0$ take, at an arrow $u\circ f_k$ (any $u$ out of $W_0$, to either object), the value $a(u)$, the extension of $a$ at $u$; at an arrow not of this form, and at $u\circ f_k$ when $P_k$ does not match $c$, the empty extension. By Lemma 4 this is well defined, and the model being full it is in the domain at $W_0$. Its transport along $f_k$ is $u\mapsto I_c(u\circ f_k)=a(u)$, that is $f_k\cdot I_c=a$: at the world $f_k$ the constants matched by $P_k$ denote $\bar a_k$.

The constants are thereby elements of the root domains, which is all that a model of $C(\Sigma)$ evaluated at the root requires: the truth value at the root of any sentence is computed from the root denotations of its constants and their transports along the arrows out of $W_0$. (Nothing at $W_1$ is used; if an interpretation there is wanted, take the empty intensions.)

**Theorem.** Witnessed Possibility, $(\exists\bar x\,P)\to\Diamond P[\bar c/\bar x]$ for pure $P$ and distinct matching constants $\bar c$, holds at the root.

*Proof.* If $\exists\bar x\,P$ is true at the root, $P$ is some $P_k$, and at the world $f_k$ the constants denote its witnesses, so $P[\bar c]$ holds there. $\square$

The other half of Logical Necessity, No Pure Contingency, fails (§3.2), so this is the exact half that the gluing by itself supplies.

## 3. Verdicts

### 3.1 Strong Actuality fails at the root and holds at $W_1$

At $W_0$ the only candidate strong atom is the true atom $\{1_{W_0}\}$, and its transport along $h$ is the set of retractions of $h$, $\{j_1,j_2\}$ (Corollary 3), which the proposition $\{j_1\}$ at $W_1$ separates; in the map this is the general argument from separated retractions. At $W_1$, the true atom $\{1_{W_1}\}$ becomes empty under every non-identity arrow out of $W_1$, none of which has a retraction (Corollary 3), so it is a strong atom there. Since a closed pure sentence's truth at an arrow depends only on the arrow's target, **the pure sentence Strong Actuality is true at exactly the worlds with target $W_1$**.

### 3.2 No Pure Contingency, Converse Witnessed Possibility, Modal Freedom fail

The closed pure sentence $\neg\mathrm{SA}$ is true at the root and false at the world $h$: No Pure Contingency fails, and with it Logical Necessity. Converse Witnessed Possibility, $\Diamond P[\bar c]\to\exists\bar x\,P$, fails for the closed instance $P:=\mathrm{SA}$: $\Diamond\mathrm{SA}$ holds at $h$ while $\mathrm{SA}$ is false at the root. Modal Freedom fails for $P:=\mathrm{SA}$, $Q:=\neg\mathrm{SA}$: both are possible, their conjunction is not.

### 3.3 Possibly Witnessed Possibility and Separated Structure fail

Let $c$ be a constant of type $t$ and $P(x):=\mathrm{SA}\wedge x\wedge\forall q\,(x\le q\vee x\le\neg q)$, "Strong Actuality holds and $x$ is a true atom". Then $\Diamond\exists x\,P$ holds at the root, witnessed at the world $h$ by $\{1_{W_1}\}$. But $P[c]$ holds at no world $u$ out of $W_0$. If the target of $u$ is $W_0$, $\mathrm{SA}$ fails there. If it is $W_1$, then $u=h\circ v$ by Lemma 4, and $c$ denotes at $u$ the transport $h\cdot(v\cdot I_c)$ of a proposition $p$ at $W_0$ along $h$, namely $\{x : x\circ h\in p\}$; since $e_i\circ h=h$, this set contains $1_{W_1}$ iff it contains $e_1$, so it is not $\{1_{W_1}\}$, the only true atom at $W_1$. Hence $\Diamond P[c]$ fails and Possibly Witnessed Possibility fails. Separated Structure implies Possibly Witnessed Possibility (recorded), so Separated Structure fails too.

### 3.4 B for pure sentences holds

Every object reaches every object: $h$ leads from $W_0$ to $W_1$ and $j_1$ back. If a closed pure sentence $P$ is true at the root, then at any world $u$ it is possible: if the target of $u$ is $W_0$, $P$ holds at $u$ itself; if it is $W_1$, $P$ holds at $j_1\circ u$. So $P\to\Box\Diamond P$.

### 3.5 The rest

- **Full-model package.** Being full, the model satisfies Rigid Comprehension, Actuality, Atomicity, Boolean Completeness, the choice principles, Tame Rigidity, Rigid Power and their necessitations (Classicism, p. 61, and the topic's general arguments for full models), and 5-necessities are two-hat necessary.
- **BF fails** at type $t$: transports along $h$ contain $1_{W_1}$ iff they contain $e_1$, so $h^t$ is not surjective (Classicism, Proposition 3.24(iii)); $h$ is not epic ($e_1\circ h=1_{W_1}\circ h$). **ND fails**, as it must given Witnessed Possibility (recorded incompatibility). **Two-hat necessities are ND-necessary fails**: $h$ has a retraction and no inverse. **Truths are 5-necessary holds**, boxed: the identities are the only isomorphisms (Corollary 3). Strong Atomicity (type $t$) fails: $h$ is not epic.
- **Signature.** Independence holds, since Witnessed Possibility implies it (recorded). No Contingency and B for sentences of $\Sigma$ fail, being incompatible with Witnessed Possibility. Maximalism fails, the pure necessities of a full model exceeding C.
- **Infinity**: with one individual the type-$e$ principles fail; with infinitely many arrows out of the root the type-$t$ ones hold.

## 4. What the model does, and the recipe

The model separates Witnessed Possibility from Separated Structure, from Possibly Witnessed Possibility and from Strong Actuality; no other model on the map has Witnessed Possibility without any of these. It puts Bacon's two derivations in Logical Combinatorialism, §4, on different footings: Independence needs only the witnessed half of Logical Necessity, while Separated Structure genuinely needs No Pure Contingency.

The recipe is general. For any category $\mathcal C$ with a root object $W_0$, the full model over $\mathcal C *_{W_0}F$ with the constants glued at the sites satisfies Witnessed Possibility at the root, by Lemma 4 and §2. Adjoining the free sites preserves retractions (Corollary 3), epimorphisms ($f_k$ is right-cancellable) and reachability between objects, so what $\mathcal C$ does to the pure principles survives, and the pure theories of the several objects break No Pure Contingency. What the recipe cannot do is separate Witnessed Possibility from what every full model satisfies; that would need domains that are not full yet closed under the gluing.
