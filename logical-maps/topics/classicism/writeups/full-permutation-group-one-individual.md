# Full action model: permutations of an infinite set, one individual

This is the variant of the model in the write-up for `full-permutation-group-infinite-set` in which $W^e$ is a singleton $\{\ast\}$ with the trivial action, the arrows still being all permutations of a countably infinite set $X$; Classicism, §3.5, p. 59, chooses the type-$e$ action in this way for its two-arrow examples. Everything in that write-up not depending on the individual domain goes through unchanged; this note records the differences.

**Lemma 1** holds verbatim: the type-$e$ action is bijective because it is the identity, and the remaining clauses concern the arrows and the propositions. **Lemma 2** is the same one-object observation. So $\Box$ND, $\Box$BF, $\Box$5, B, the necessitations of Atomicity, Actuality, Boolean Completeness, Rigid Comprehension, Gallin Extensional Comprehension, Functionality, Plenitude, Functional Choice and Relational Choice, No Pure Contingency and Converse Witnessed Possibility hold by the arguments given there. The propositional domain is still $\mathcal P(G)$ with $G$ infinite, so the Axiom of Infinity at type $t$ holds. The Fregean Axiom, Extensionality and the Distinctness-preserving collapse fail as there.

**Type $e$.** There is exactly one individual at every arrow and identity is literal, so the first numeral holds of $\lambda u^e\, .\,\top$: the Axiom of Infinity at type $e$ fails, the second instance of the Infinity Schema at type $e$ is false, and Possible Infinity at type $e$ fails since every arrow sees the same domain. Possibility Maximalism (pure) fails for the C-consistent sentence $\exists xy\, .\,x\ne y$, false at every arrow, and Distinctness Maximalism (pure) fails for the true unprovable pure identity $(\forall xy\, .\,x=y)=\top$.

**Constants.** The record interprets Σ differently from the natural-action model. One designated relational constant $c$ of type $\tau=\bar\sigma\to t$ denotes $\lambda\bar x\, .\,a$ with $a=\{1\}$, the unique true atom; every other relational constant denotes $\top$ of its type, Σ being assumed to contain at least one such constant; every individual constant denotes $\ast$.

- *No Contingency (signature Σ) fails.* The Σ-sentence $\forall\bar x\, .\,c\bar x$ denotes $\bigcap_{\bar b}a=a$, which is true at $1$ and is not $G$, so it is not necessary. B for sentences of Σ nevertheless holds, by B.
- *Witnessed Possibility fails*, and with it Separated Structure, Distinctness Maximalism (signature Σ), Possibility Maximalism (signature Σ) and Logical Necessity. Take the pure formula $x=\top_\tau$, witnessed by $\top_\tau$. By Lemma 1(4), $[c=\top_\tau]=\emptyset$ since $\lambda\bar x\, .\,a\ne\lambda\bar x\, .\,G$, so $\Diamond(c=\top_\tau)$ is false.
- *Independence (signature Σ) fails* through any other relational constant $c'$, which denotes what the closed pure term $\top_{\tau'}$ denotes. The designated constant itself does not refute it: closed pure terms denote entities fixed by every arrow (Lemma 2), whereas $i\cdot a=\{i^{-1}\}$, so $a$, and $\lambda\bar x\, .\,a$ with it, is fixed only by the identity arrow.
- *Modal Freedom (signature Σ) holds.* Its instances are $\Diamond P[\bar c]\land\Diamond Q[\bar d]\to\Diamond(P[\bar c]\land Q[\bar d])$ with $\bar c$ and $\bar d$ disjoint tuples of distinct constants. The designated constant occurs in at most one of them, say not in $\bar d$. Every constant in $\bar d$ then denotes a fixed point of the action ($\top$, or $\ast$), so $Q[\bar d]$ denotes a fixed point of $W^t$ by Lemma 2, that is $\emptyset$ or $G$. If $\Diamond Q[\bar d]$ is true it denotes $G$, and $P[\bar c]\land Q[\bar d]$ denotes $P[\bar c]$, which is possible by hypothesis.
- *Converse Witnessed Possibility holds*, by the argument of the natural-action write-up, which uses no property of the interpretation.
