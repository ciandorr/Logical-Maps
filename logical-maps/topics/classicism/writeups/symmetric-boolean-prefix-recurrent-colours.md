# Symmetric ideally-full model: atomless Boolean prefix with recurrent colour subalgebras

Construction and proofs by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, research note
*Symmetric ideally-full model: an atomless Boolean prefix with recurrent colour subalgebras* of
26 October 2026. Checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by
Cian Dorr, 29 October 2026.

At its evaluation point $1_A$, this model satisfies:

- Rigid Comprehension, at every relational type, hence Tame Rigidity, Boolean Completeness and
  Actuality;
- □BF;
- □Rigid Power.

It fails:

- Weakly Rigid Power and □Tame Rigidity, both at type $et$;
- Relational Choice.

So Rigid Comprehension with □BF and □Rigid Power does not give Weakly Rigid Power, and Rigid
Comprehension is not necessary here. This strengthens symmetric-recurrent-colours, which has
only Tame Rigidity in place of Rigid Comprehension. As there, a tail of objects with finitely
many colours lets every object return to a finite-colour one. What is new is a *prefix* object
$A$ in front of the tail, whose arrows into the tail have dense kernels. That is what lets
rigid relations at $A$ lift.

## Boolean algebra facts

Let $B$ be the countable atomless Boolean algebra. Every nonzero interval $[0,b]$ is again one.
Write $\langle P\rangle$ for the subalgebra generated by a set $P$; for finite $P$ it is finite.

**(a) Finite isomorphisms extend.** An isomorphism between finite subalgebras of two countable
atomless algebras extends to an isomorphism of the algebras. Match the atoms, and join
isomorphisms of the corresponding intervals, built by back and forth.

**(b) Finite homomorphisms extend surjectively.** Let $A_0$ be a finite subalgebra of $B$ and
$f:A_0\to B'$ a homomorphism into a countable atomless $B'$. Then $f$ extends to a surjective
homomorphism $B\to B'$. Take an isomorphism $[0,a]\to[0,f(a)]$ for each atom $a$ of $A_0$ with
$f(a)\ne0$, the zero map for the others, and join them. The result is onto because the nonzero
$f(a)$ partition $1$.

**(c) Partitions lift.** If $h:B\to B'$ is onto, $s\in B$, and $b_1,\dots,b_r$ partition $h(s)$,
some partition $a_1,\dots,a_r$ of $s$ has $h(a_i)=b_i$. Lift, disjointify, and add the remainder to
one piece. A piece with nonzero image is nonzero.

The kernel of $h$ is *dense* when every nonzero $b$ has some nonzero $z\le b$ with $h(z)=0$.

**(d) Dense-kernel surjections.** These exist between any two countable atomless algebras. On a
free presentation, send the even generators onto generators of the target and the odd ones to
$0$. If $b\ne0$ depends on finitely many generators, $b\land a$ is a nonzero kernel element for a
fresh odd generator $a$. Composing a dense-kernel surjection on either side with a surjection
gives another one, since the kernel only grows.

**(e) Lifting by an automorphism.** Let $h,h':B\to B'$ be onto, with $\ker h$ dense, agreeing on
a finite subalgebra $P$. Then for every finite subalgebra $M\supseteq P$ some automorphism $u$ of
$B$ fixing $P$ pointwise has $hu=h'$ on $M$.

*Proof.* Work in each atom $s$ of $P$, with $m_1,\dots,m_r$ the atoms of $M$ below it. We need a
partition of $s$ into nonzero $n_i$ with $h(n_i)=h'(m_i)$.

- If $h(s)=0$, any partition into $r$ nonzero pieces works.
- Otherwise, take a nonzero kernel element $z<s$.
  - Split $z$ into nonzero pieces for the indices with $h'(m_i)=0$.
  - Lift the partition of $h(s)=h(s\setminus z)$ by the nonzero $h'(m_i)$ to $s\setminus z$, by (c).
  - If no index has zero image, add $z$ to one of the pieces.

The map $m_i\mapsto n_i$ is an isomorphism of finite subalgebras fixing $P$. Extend it by (a).
$\blacksquare$

## The model

**Objects.**

- There is a *prefix* object $A$, whose base is $B$.
- There are *tail* objects $V_m$ for $m\ge0$, and $V_\infty$, with countable atomless bases and
  *colour* subalgebras:
  - $V_m$ is free on $c_0,\dots,c_{m-1},d_0,d_1,\dots$, and $C_m:=\langle c_0,\dots,c_{m-1}\rangle$,
    which is finite. In particular $C_0=\{0,1\}$.
  - $V_\infty$ is free on $c_0,c_1,\dots,d_0,d_1,\dots$, and $C_\infty:=\langle c_0,c_1,\dots\rangle$.

**Arrows.**

- $A\to A$: the automorphisms of $B$.
- $A\to V$, for $V$ in the tail: the surjective homomorphisms with dense kernel.
- $V\to W$, both in the tail: the surjective homomorphisms $f$ with $f[C_V]\subseteq C_W$.
- No arrows go from the tail to $A$.

These are closed under composition, by (d).

**Symmetries and domains.**

- The symmetry group at $A$ is $\operatorname{Aut}(B)$.
- At a tail object $V$, it is the automorphisms with $f[C_V]=C_V$.
- Every object carries the ideal of finite sets.
- The domains are the symmetric ideally-full ones (Dorr, *Boolean Completeness does not imply
  Rigid Comprehension*, Definitions 15–18).
- The individuals are the elements of the bases.
- Evaluation is at $1_A$.

Every entity $a$ is pinned down by some finite set, so if two arrows with the same target agree
on it, they transport $a$ identically; enlarging the set, we may take it to be a finite
subalgebra. Write $h\cdot a$ for transport and $F_h$ for the value of a relation $F$ at $h$.

**Two facts of C.**

- An *exact-image* relation, one with $F_{kh}=k[F_h]$ for all composable $h,k$, is rigid: it is
  persistent, and whatever necessarily holds of its members at $h$ holds of every member at $kh$.
- Coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical ([[coextensive-weakly-rigid-identical]]).

## □BF

Every arrow is surjective on bases. So, at every object, an entity at the target of an arrow $h$,
pinned down by a finite $T$, is the transport along $h$ of an entity built from a finite section
of $h$ over $T$ (the group argument barcan-surjective). This gives BF at every type and every
object, so □BF holds at $A$.

## Rigid Comprehension

**Admission lemma.** Let $D$ be a set of tuples of entities at $A$, invariant under the
automorphisms fixing a finite subalgebra $P$ pointwise. Then $G_h:=h[D]$ is in the domain,
pinned down by $P$, so it is an exact-image relation and rigid.

*Proof.* Let $h,h'$ have the same target and agree on $P$, and let $a\in D$ be pinned down by a
finite $M\supseteq P$.

- If the target is $A$, then $u:=h^{-1}h'$ fixes $P$.
- If it is in the tail, (e) gives $u$ fixing $P$ with $hu=h'$ on $M$.

Either way, $h'\cdot a=h\cdot(u\cdot a)\in h[D]$, by invariance. By symmetry of the argument,
$h[D]=h'[D]$. And $(gh)[D]=g[h[D]]$, so the relation is symmetric. $\blacksquare$

Now let $F$ be a relation at $A$, pinned down by a finite subalgebra $P$. An automorphism fixing
$P$ fixes $F$, so it maps $F$'s extension $D=F_1$ onto itself. The lemma gives a rigid relation
coextensive with $F$. So Rigid Comprehension holds at every relational type. Moreover, by the
second fact of C, **every rigid $F$ at $A$ is the exact-image relation $h\mapsto h[F_1]$**.

## Rigid Power

**A single kernel element.** Fix an entrance $h:A\to V$ and a finite subalgebra $P$ of $B$. Let
$z\in\ker h$ be the join of:

- every atom $s$ of $P$ with $h(s)=0$;
- for each other atom $s$, a nonzero kernel element $z_s<s$.

Put $S:=\langle P,z\rangle$.

**Normalisation.** For every finite $M\supseteq P$, some automorphism $u$ fixing $P$ has $hu=h$
on $M$, and every element of $L:=\langle u[M],S\rangle$ in $\ker h$ lies below $z$.

*Proof.* In an atom $s$ of $P$ with $h(s)\ne0$, choose the partition $n_i$ of (e), with $h'=h$,
using $z\land s$ as the reserved kernel piece. Then every atom of $L$ below $s$ is either below $z$
or disjoint from $z$ with nonzero image. Atoms of $P$ with $h(s)=0$ lie below $z$. $\blacksquare$

So every tuple $a$ has a *normalised* conjugate $b=u\cdot a$ with $h\cdot b=h\cdot a$, pinned
down by an $L\supseteq S$ whose $h$-kernel lies below $z$. The element $z$ depends only on $h$ and
$P$, not on $a$.

**Lifting at a finite-colour object.** Let $F$ be rigid at $A$, $h:A\to V_m$, and $Y\le h\cdot F$
rigid at $V_m$. Then some rigid $X\le F$ has $h\cdot X=Y$.

*Proof.*

1. **Setup.** Let $D:=F_1$, and let $T$ be a finite set pinning $Y$ down. Choose a finite
   subalgebra $P$ that pins $F$ down and has $T\cup C_m\subseteq h[P]$. Take $z$ and $S$ as above.
2. **The relation $X$.** Let $E$ be the union of the orbits, under the automorphisms fixing $S$,
   of the normalised $b\in D$ with $h\cdot b\in Y_1$. It lies in $D$ and is invariant. So by the
   admission lemma it gives a rigid $X\le F$ with $X_h=h[E]$.
3. **$h[E]\subseteq Y_1$.**
   - Let $b$ be such a tuple, pinned down by $L$ as above, and let $u$ fix $S$.
   - Put $v_0(h(l)):=h(u(l))$ for $l\in L$. This is well defined: if $h(l)=h(l')$, then $l\mathbin\triangle l'\le z$, which $u$ fixes.
   - $v_0$ is a homomorphism on the finite algebra $h[L]$ that fixes $h[P]$, hence fixes $T$ and $C_m$.
   - By (b) it extends to a surjective endomorphism $v$ of $V_m$. As $v$ fixes $C_m$ it is an arrow; as it fixes $T$, it fixes $Y$.
   - Since $hu=vh$ on $L$, we get $h\cdot(u\cdot b)=v\cdot(h\cdot b)\in Y_1$, by persistence of $Y$.
4. **$Y_1\subseteq h[E]$.** Given $y\in Y_1$, we have $y=h\cdot a$ for some $a\in D$, since $Y\le h\cdot F$ and $F_h=h[D]$. Normalising $a$ gives $b\in E$ with $h\cdot b=y$.
5. **Conclusion.** So $h\cdot X$ and $Y$ are rigid and coextensive, hence identical. $\blacksquare$

**Finite-colour approximation.** For finite $L$ in the base of $V_\infty$, there are tail arrows
$c:V_\infty\to V_m$ and $k:V_m\to V_\infty$ such that $kc$ fixes $L$ pointwise.

- List the finitely many generators $L$ depends on, $m$ of them colour generators, generating
  $C'$.
- Let $k$ be an isomorphism of the bases carrying $C_m$ onto $C'$.
- Let $r$ be the endomorphism of $V_\infty$ that fixes the listed generators and sends the other
  colour generators to $0$. On the unlisted $d$-generators, choose $r$ so that their images
  generate everything, which makes $r$ surjective.
- Then $r[C_\infty]\subseteq C'$, so $c:=k^{-1}r$ is a tail arrow, and $kc=r$.

**Rigid Power at $A$.** Let $F$ be rigid and $R_F:=\lambda X\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le
F$. It is persistent. For weak inextensibility, let $Z$, pinned down by a finite $N$, hold
necessarily of every current instance of $R_F$. Let $h$ be an arrow from $A$, and $Y\le h\cdot F$ rigid at its target $V$.

- **If $V$ is $A$ or a $V_m$:** lift $Y$ to a rigid $X\le F$ with $h\cdot X=Y$, by inverting $h$ or by the lifting lemma. Then $Z_h(Y)$.
- **If $V=V_\infty$:**
  - Let $T$ pin $Y$ down, and take $c,k$ with $kc$ fixing $T\cup h[N]$.
  - The arrow $ch$ is an entrance, and $c\cdot Y\le(ch)\cdot F$ is rigid. So the lifting lemma gives a rigid $X\le F$ with $(ch)\cdot X=c\cdot Y$.
  - For $j:=kch$, we have $j\cdot X=(kc)\cdot Y=Y$, and $j$ agrees with $h$ on $N$.
  - So $Z_j(Y)$, and $Z_h=Z_j$.

Hence $R_F$ is weakly rigid, and by Tame Rigidity at $A$ it is rigid. So Rigid Power holds at
every type.

With □BF the universal relation of each type is rigid, and Rigid Power at it says that the
property of being rigid is rigid, which is Rigid Rigidity. So Rigid Power is necessary
([[rigid-rigidity-and-rigid-power-imply-necessary-rigid-power]]).

## The colour property, and the failures

At $V_\infty$ let $U$ be the property with $U_h:=C_W$ for every arrow $h:V_\infty\to W$. It is
symmetric, pinned down by $\varnothing$, and persistent, because arrows preserve colour.

**$U$ is weakly inextensible.** Let $Z$, pinned down by a finite $P$, hold necessarily of every
member of $C_\infty$. Let $h:V_\infty\to W$ and $y\in C_W$.

- Choose finitely many generators expressing $P$, and a fresh colour generator $x$.
- Let $j$ agree with $h$ on the chosen generators, send $x$ to $y$ and the other colour generators into $C_W$, and use the remaining $d$-generators to make $j$ surjective.
- Then $j$ is an arrow agreeing with $h$ on $P$. So $Z_j(jx)$, that is $Z_j(y)$, and $Z_h=Z_j$.

**$U$ is not rigid.**

- Send every colour generator to $0$ to get an arrow $l:V_\infty\to V_0$. There $l\cdot U$ has the
  extension $\{0,1\}$.
- The disjunction $H$ of the haecceities of $0$ and $1$ holds necessarily of both members.
- But along an isomorphism $k:V_0\to V_\infty$, which is an arrow, $l\cdot U$ has the infinite
  extension $C_\infty$, while $H$ has extension $\{0,1\}$.

So $l\cdot U$ is not weakly inextensible.

There is an entrance $A\to V_\infty$, by (d). So □Tame Rigidity fails at $A$, at type $et$.

**Weakly Rigid Power fails** at the universal property of type $et$, which is rigid. Its weakly
rigid subproperties at $A$ are rigid, by Tame Rigidity. So the property of being rigid holds
necessarily of every current instance of the weak power. But $U$, at $V_\infty$, is a weakly rigid
subproperty that is not rigid. So the weak power is not weakly inextensible.

## Other verdicts

**Actuality.** The proposition true at the arrows into $A$ is pinned down by $\varnothing$ and is
the symmetry group at $A$. So it is an atom that entails every truth. Its transport to every tail
object is $\bot$.

**Relational Choice fails**, by the group argument. For a finite $N$, the property of lying
outside $\langle h[N]\rangle$ is symmetric and pinned down by $N$. An element $y\notin\langle
N\rangle$ splits some atom $s$ of $\langle N\rangle$, and an automorphism exchanging $y\land s$
with $s\setminus y$ inside $[0,s]$ fixes $\langle N\rangle$ and moves $y$.

**Scope.** The arguments using individuals are stated for the base as individuals. The generated
variant with one individual is not covered by them.
