Simple Relative Expectation and L¹ Continuity imply Relative Expectation

Claim. Rich Outcomes + Simple Relative Expectation + L¹ Continuity imply Relative Expectation.

All variables in this proof are in the real utility chart; write their utilities as X,YX,Y to simplify notation. Fix D=X−YD=X-Y with 𝔼|D|<∞\mathbb E|D|<\infty, and write m=𝔼Dm=\mathbb E D. Simple functions are dense in L1L^1, so choose simple qnq_n with 𝔼|qn−D|→0\mathbb E|q_n-D|\to0. Define

dn=qn+m−𝔼qn.d_n=q_n+m-\mathbb E q_n.

These are still simple, satisfy 𝔼dn=m\mathbb E d_n=m, and obey

𝔼|dn−D|≤𝔼|qn−D|+|m−𝔼qn|≤2𝔼|qn−D|→0.\mathbb E|d_n-D|\leq \mathbb E|q_n-D|+|m-\mathbb E q_n| \leq 2\mathbb E|q_n-D|\longrightarrow0.

Rich Outcomes supplies every finite level needed to form the measurable gambles Xn=Y+dnX_n=Y+d_n.

First suppose m≥0m\geq0. Because Xn−Y=dnX_n-Y=d_n is simple with mean mm, Simple Relative Expectation gives Xn≽YX_n\succeq Y for every nn. The actual coupling satisfies 𝔼|Xn−X|→0\mathbb E|X_n-X|\to0. The upper-section clause of L¹ Continuity therefore gives X≽YX\succeq Y.

This proves the nonnegative-mean direction for every integrable-difference pair. For the converse, suppose m<0m<0 and put c=−m/2>0c=-m/2>0. We have

𝔼[Y−(X+c)]=−m−c=−m/2>0.\mathbb E[Y-(X+c)]=-m-c=-m/2>0.

The direction just proved gives Y≽X+cY\succeq X+c. Simple Relative Expectation, applied to the constant difference cc, gives X+c≻XX+c\succ X: its forward weak comparison holds and its reverse weak comparison fails. If X≽YX\succeq Y, transitivity would give X≽X+cX\succeq X+c, a contradiction. Hence $X\not\succeq Y$ whenever m<0m<0.

Together these establish exactly X≽Y⇔𝔼(X−Y)≥0X\succeq Y\iff\mathbb E(X-Y)\geq0.

The mean correction is essential for obtaining approximants on the required side when m=0m=0. The positive perturbation handles strictness without assuming lower-section closure. No Totality, Stochastic Equivalence, Mixture Independence, reflection or affine symmetry is used. Rich Outcomes supplies intermediate real utility levels; it is not silently used to exclude outcomes outside the chart.
